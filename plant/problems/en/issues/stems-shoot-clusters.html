Clusters of weak shoots at one bud

Many thin shoots crowd out of one bud. A single dark swelling on the stem belongs with the gall guide page.

What to look for

Compare the cluster with normal branching and note nearby pruning cuts or wounds.

Possible causes and next steps

Bud stimulation, pests and disease can all affect branching. Photograph both the whole plant and the cluster for identification.

How to tell similar problems apart

Several shoots emerging just below a pruning cut can be a normal response. A dense tuft of unusually short, thin shoots arising away from a cut deserves closer inspection.

Part the shoots enough to see their bases. Several upright shoots in a row along a limb may each have a separate origin. A tight tuft can emerge from one short section of wood. Take one photo from the side that shows where the cluster starts and another showing how much of the canopy is affected.

What to do now

Trace the shoots back to their starting point. Record any old cut, swelling or graft union nearby, and keep the cluster out of material used for propagation.

Look for an old pruning stub or storm break next to the cluster. If it appeared with the first growth after damage and its leaves develop normally, give the branch time to show its growth pattern. On a grafted shrub, compare shoots emerging below the graft with those above; the rootstock can produce shoots with quite different leaves.

What to check next

Compare the next flush on the affected and unaffected branches. If clusters recur away from cuts or appear on more branches, seek a host-specific diagnosis.

Record whether the next growth has normal-sized leaves, shortened shoots or another tight cluster. If a tuft returns in the same spot or similar tufts develop elsewhere without pruning, keep a representative shoot for professional inspection and ask how to submit it before cutting.

Check for an old cut at the center

Look inside the cluster for an old pruning cut. Several vigorous upright shoots around a cut may be a regrowth response. Repeated clusters of thin weak shoots from intact buds on unpruned branches warrant a different investigation. Photograph the center and its parent branch before thinning the shoots; the point of origin is easily lost once they are cut.
