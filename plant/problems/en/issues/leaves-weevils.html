Weevils and notched leaf margins

The photograph shows no notched leaf edge, only the beetle and a grass stem. Notches have to be found on a broad leaf. This frame is not evidence of a notch.

Inspect the specimen

Recognize the snout: the head projects, and the antennae arise on it. Then look for a semicircular notch on a rose or rhododendron leaf edge. Record the notch and the beetle together only when they are on the same specimen.

Check similar symptoms

A sawfly scrapes a leaf into a translucent patch and does not leave this semicircular notch. If you see notches and no beetle by day, look again in the evening.

Choose a response

If the same snouted beetle is present in the evening, remove it. The notch itself is not a reason to cut off the whole leaf.

Track the next change

When new leaves stop gaining notches, the old notches remain on older leaves. Do not record an old notch as fresh injury.

Host-specific check

Use this page for a broad leaf edge, and only when the notch is actually there. A beetle merely resting on grass, with sound leaves, is an insect note alone.

Match fresh edge notches to nighttime activity

Photograph a notched evergreen leaf in daylight and inspect that shoot after dark with a flashlight. Some adult root weevils feed at night and hide by day. If a container plant also wilts, inspect its root ball separately; leaf notches alone cannot establish larval root injury.
