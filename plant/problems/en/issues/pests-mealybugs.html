Mealybugs

Cottony insects in leaf joints

This is one possible explanation. Compare the signs below with those on your plant.

What to look for

Look into leaf axils for wax-coated insects and sticky honeydew.

Possible causes and next steps

These signs suggest mealybugs. Isolate the plant, wipe off small groups and recheck new growth.

How to tell similar problems apart

Wax-covered insects collect in leaf joints. Fuzzy deposits without a segmented insect underneath may have another cause.

Trace a white patch from its outer edge towards the centre. Under magnification, a live mealybug has a distinct body beneath the wax; accumulated lint or mineral deposits do not. Inspect the sheltered side of a leaf stalk where it joins the stem, around flower stalks and beneath the lip of a pot. Sticky residue on a shelf is a cue to inspect the plant above it.

What to do now

Separate the pot from adjoining foliage and inspect sheltered joints and pot rims. Lift small colonies with a damp swab.

Move an affected pot far enough that leaves no longer touch neighbouring pots. On sturdy foliage, clean one small accessible group first and check the surface for damage before cleaning the rest. Avoid pulling apart a tightly furled new leaf to reach hidden insects. Instead, note its position and inspect it once open. Check nearby pots separately so moving one plant does not conceal an established second colony.

What to check next

Reinspect previously clean shoots. New cottony clusters there matter more than wax left at an old feeding site.
