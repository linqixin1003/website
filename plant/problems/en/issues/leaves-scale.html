Scale insects

Shell-like bumps on stems or leaves

This is one possible explanation. Compare the signs below with those on your plant.

What to look for

Inspect bumps along veins and stems with a lens and distinguish surface shells from the plant tissue.

Inspect the plant before washing or moving it. Start where the damage looks freshest and turn the leaf over without shaking the shoot. Include the attachment to the stem in your inspection. A hand lens or a close phone photo can reveal detail that a picture of the whole plant misses.

Possible causes and next steps

Scale insects can settle on stems and leaves. Separate the affected pot and inspect neighboring plants.

How to tell similar problems apart

Inspect several bumps with a hand lens. Regularly spaced pores in bark are normal; a scale insect has a distinct outline on the surface.

Inspect the sides of stems and main veins where bumps repeat in clusters. On one suspect bump, gently check whether a separate covering or body lifts away. Natural corky tissue is continuous with the bark. Sticky honeydew helps locate some scales, but its absence does not exclude them.

What to do now

Inspect leaf veins, petioles and woody joints. Gently lift one suspect scale on a sturdy stem rather than scraping the entire plant.

After cleaning a small stem section, photograph its limits. This makes newly settled bumps easier to distinguish from old shells elsewhere on the stem. Save a close-up of any occupant alongside a picture showing the injury on the plant.

What to check next

Empty covers may remain attached. Check for new insects on untreated growth before repeating any treatment.
