Striped beetles chew cucumber seedlings and gather inside blossoms

Striped cucumber beetles may arrive while cucumber and squash seedlings have very little leaf area to spare. On larger plants the same insects can hide deep inside open flowers. The host, stage of growth and markings of the beetle help interpret a find.

Where to look

Inspect both seed leaves, their edges and the first true leaf. Later in the season, peer into a newly opened blossom without pulling it apart and check the developing fruit nearby for feeding scars.

What confirms it

The striped adult is an elongated yellow beetle with a dark head and three black lengthwise stripes. Look closely at the length of the stripes before identifying any yellow beetle in a squash flower.

Similar damage

Adult western corn rootworms can visit cucurbit flowers too. On striped female rootworm beetles the dark marks often fail to reach the wing-cover tips; an insect on a flower alone is weak evidence.

What to do

Protect clean seedlings with an insect barrier and secure the edges. Remove or open the covering when blossoms need insect pollination. On a small patch check the plants inside before closing the cover.

Follow-up

Recheck new leaves as seedlings expand. Record wilt separately from chewing; cucumber beetles can transmit bacterial wilt, but a wilted cucumber still needs examination for watering and stem problems.
