Periodical cicadas and dying twig tips

The photograph shows the red-eyed adult, not a dead twig. Look on the tree for egg slits and brown tips.

Periodical cicadas can leave scattered brown flags in a leafy tree after adults disappear. Females cut slots in slender woody branches to lay eggs. Tissue beyond a badly weakened spot may wilt or break even while the rest of the canopy looks healthy.

Where to look

Trace a brown cluster back toward living foliage. Turn the branch and inspect its bark for a row of narrow slits, especially along a twig roughly the thickness of a pencil.

What confirms it

Look nearby for hollow nymphal skins attached to trunks. An earlier chorus of adults with reddish eyes and orange wing veins supports a local emergence, but inspect the injured twig itself.

Similar damage

A drought-stressed tree may wilt more generally. Egg-laying injury tends to produce a sharp change along a single twig, with the green section below the cut and brown leaves farther out.

What to do

An established tree usually tolerates scattered flags. Protect a newly planted susceptible tree with finely meshed netting before a predicted local emergence, securing gaps so adults cannot reach the twigs.

Follow-up

After adults disappear remove the cover, inspect the leader and record any break. A tree with just one young central shoot deserves closer follow-up than a mature tree with several flagged side twigs.

Check a flagged twig below the egg slits for an intact junction with its parent branch. A dead distal tip appearing after the emergence may be the delayed result of egg laying. Once adults are gone, covering the tree cannot protect that already injured section.
