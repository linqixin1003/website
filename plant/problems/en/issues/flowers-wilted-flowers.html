Yellow petals have lost their shape

The yellow petals fold inward and overlap in loose wrinkles. The short stretch of stalk visible beneath them is green. The photograph does not show the roots or establish why the petals have wilted.

Inspect the specimen

Look along the stalk without squeezing it. Note whether just the bloom droops, or the stalk has softened and fallen at soil level.

Check similar symptoms

A spent bloom can hang as the petals dry, while the stalk stays straight. Rot at the base bends the stalk near the soil, not at the neck.

Choose a response

Leave a newly wilted bloom in place and check it again in the cool morning. If the petals regain firmness, keep the flower. When the petals have clearly faded and dried, you may remove the spent bloom while leaving the green stalk and leaves.

Track the next change

Compare the oldest bloom with one that has just opened. Drying confined to fading older petals supports normal ageing; younger flowers drooping at the same time call for a moisture and heat check.

Host-specific check

Compare an older bloom with a newly opened flower of the same kind. A naturally nodding bloom can still have firm petals; look for a change in their texture and shape.

Check the neck beneath the drooping flower before watering a bulb in bloom. If only that neck has folded and the leaves and substrate are already moist, more water will not straighten the damaged stalk. Check the next unopened flower separately.

One bulb flower droops every afternoon. Is the bulb failing?

Look again in the cool morning before changing its care. If the bloom regains its posture and the unopened buds and leaves stay firm, check afternoon heat exposure and soil moisture at root depth. If a single neck stays sharply folded, support the pot when moving it and let the remaining foliage grow. If several stalks fall at soil level and their bases soften while the mix stays wet, stop adding water and inspect the crown separately.
