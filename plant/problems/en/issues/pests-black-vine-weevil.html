Black vine weevil on ornamental plants

The photograph is a close view of the adult, not a notched leaf or a root. Check the leaves and the roots for those injuries.

Black vine weevils cause two kinds of injury in ornamental plantings. Adults chew the edges of leaves at night. The pale larvae develop in the soil, where feeding can damage roots, especially in a nursery pot.

Where to look

Choose a leaf with fresh, rounded notches and inspect its edges after dark. Check the stem and pot rim as well: an adult disturbed during inspection may drop rather than stay on the leaf.

What confirms it

If a container plant also loses vigor, slide out its root ball carefully. Look among the outer roots for cream-colored, curved, legless grubs with brown heads and for roots showing active chewing.

Similar damage

Leafcutter bees usually remove a cleaner, larger arc. Leaf notching alone does not establish underground injury: inspect roots before associating severe wilt with the weevil.

What to do

Handpick adults at night on a small collection. Examine neighboring containers and incoming nursery stock before bringing them alongside unaffected susceptible plants.

Follow-up

Mark the latest leaves and check their margins for new bites. Compare root recovery and shoot growth separately, since cosmetic leaf damage and root loss have quite different consequences.

Inspect a declining pot alongside a vigorous one of the same kind. Where roots remain plentiful but leaf edges are freshly notched, search for adults after dark. If fine roots have disappeared and grubs are present, picking adults from the foliage leaves the immediate root problem unresolved.
