Barley heads emerge as dark powder

A barley head can appear dark just as surrounding heads emerge green. Inspect it early, before wind removes the powder and leaves only the central axis.

What to look for

Compare heads at emergence, while healthy barley nearby is still green. In loose smut, tissues that should form flowers and kernels have been replaced by dark masses following the shape of the head.

Check the pattern

Look for a bare central axis on older affected stems. The dark material is exposed and disperses, leaving the axis behind. A few dark bodies projecting from otherwise formed kernels suggest a different problem.

What to do

Photograph the affected head in place and estimate the number of damaged heads in several parts of the stand. An already blackened head cannot go on to produce grain.

When to check again

Review the seed source before the next sowing. Infection can be carried inside apparently sound seed. Ask the local cereal advisory service about certified seed and locally suitable seed-health measures.

Distinguish an old bare head

Look for an earlier-stage head on a neighbouring stem before identifying loose smut from a bare axis alone. A head damaged by birds or mechanical stripping can also lose grain; intact dark masses on comparable heads make the interpretation much stronger.
