Orange pustules on chestnut bark

A single chestnut branch dying above a patch of rough bark is worth tracing to its base. Orange structures at that exact point are more informative than scattered brown leaves elsewhere.

Inspect the plant

Confirm the tree is a chestnut. Inspect sunken or split bark below a flag of brown leaves for orange pinpoints or orange spore tendrils in damp weather. Photograph bark on the edge of the suspected canker and the full affected limb. For a multistemmed chestnut, indicate which stems come from the same base.

Distinguish similar signs

On young smooth bark a canker can look orange; rough old bark can hide it. Shoots sprouting below an affected section help locate the interrupted stem.

Choose a response

Avoid carrying suspect bark or scion wood to other plantings. Contact a local tree diagnostic service about sampling a new suspect infection.

Follow up

Map affected stems and inspect shoots below each canker next season. Fresh basal shoots indicate survival below the lesion but can also become infected.

A detail to record

Verify the host before interpreting an orange canker. True chestnuts have simple toothed leaves; horse chestnuts have several leaflets radiating from one point and need a different diagnostic pathway.

What shoots below a chestnut canker tell you

Look for vigorous shoots below the damaged bark while also inspecting the crown above it. New growth low on the stem can coexist with a declining upper crown. Record the position of the new shoots relative to the lesion and the presence of live buds above it. For a trunk or major limb, send both views for assessment.
