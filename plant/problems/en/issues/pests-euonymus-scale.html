Identifying euonymus scale on leaves and twigs

The photograph is one dark green leaf carrying many white dashes and brown covers. It does not show a yellow leaf or a stem.

Inspect the specimen

The white marks are narrow, and the brown covers are rounder, some with a paler center. Turn the leaf and follow the stalk to a still-green twig, counting dashes and covers separately.

Check similar symptoms

Yellow flecks have to be found on another leaf. This photograph is not a substitute for them. A white powder that wipes off has none of these fixed covers.

Choose a response

Part a dense shrub so the shaded side is visible. If only a few leaves are crowded with covers, bag those leaves.

Track the next change

After the new leaves expand, count dashes and covers on the new growth. Empty covers can remain on old leaves.

Host-specific check

Identify the shrub first. Wintercreeper and other euonymus are common hosts. On a different shrub, check its host record and compare the insects on leaves and twigs rather than relying on leaf thickness or color.

Use a hand lens on a clean shoot beside a heavily crusted one. Tiny mobile crawlers crossing onto the clean bark identify a chance to target the exposed stage. A stationary patch of bleached covers on an old stem cannot tell you whether that opportunity is still open.

On low-growing wintercreeper, inspect the older stems underneath the leafy mat before discarding a sample that has only yellow dots. A leaf tip clipped from the sunny surface may show feeding injury but omit the sheltered woody stems carrying the most obvious female covers.
