Raised oval mines spot the surface of apple leaves

Spotted tentiform leafminer larvae feed between the surfaces of an apple leaf. As the mine matures, the leaf pulls into a small raised tent. The apple itself is not the feeding site, even when a leaf bearing mines touches the fruit.

Where to look

Hold an affected leaf horizontally and view it from one side. A mature oval mine can rise along its long axis; turn it over and compare the upper speckling with the contracted lower surface.

What confirms it

Look for a distinct boundary between hollowed tissue and the rest of the green blade. If one leaf is expendable, carefully opening a mature mine can show its chamber even after the occupant has left.

Similar damage

A serpentine leafmine forms a winding line, while a surface spot lacks a hollow chamber. Compare several stages because an early, flatter mine may not yet show the finished tent shape.

What to do

A few mines on a vigorous home apple tree are usually tolerable. Inspect other leaves throughout the canopy before removing foliage or deciding the tree needs treatment.

Follow-up

Follow the proportion of leaves mined and any early leaf loss. Keep fruit injuries as a separate observation; a larva tunneling into the apple requires a different identification.
