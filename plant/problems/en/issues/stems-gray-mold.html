Gray-brown fuzzy growth on stems

The photograph is an upright stem with a gray-brown mold on the middle and a dry leaf at the top. The mold sits on the stem surface.

Inspect the specimen

See whether the mold is growing out from a wound, a dead flower, or tissue that is already dead. Under the mold, note whether the stem is still firm or has gone soft.

Check similar symptoms

Powdery mildew is a dry white film, usually on a leaf that is still alive. This layer is gray-brown, on the stem, and continuous with a dead leaf.

Choose a response

Cut back to firm stem below the mold and bag the piece. Do not leave that stem in a damp mass of foliage.

Track the next change

If a neighboring stem shows the same gray mold only at a joint, keep this page.

Host-specific check

The photograph is a soft stem such as a tomato. Do not match a black patch on woody bark to this stem mold.

Check where a spent flower rests against the stem

Lift away a loose spent flower and inspect its contact point. Gray growth confined to a dead petal is a different finding from a soft brown lesion on the supporting stem. After removing the petal, check whether that point dries and stays firm. If softening spreads or the tip above wilts, record the lesion length and recent overhead watering.

Does one moldy flower cluster warrant cutting the plant back?

Separate spent petals, unopened buds and the living stalk in your check. Remove loose flowers when dry and avoid dropping pieces into the next cluster. Give crowded stems space for air to circulate. If the next buds open normally, continue routine cleanup. Softening unopened buds together with a spreading stem lesion calls for closer diagnosis.
