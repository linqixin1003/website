Brown spot

Brown patches on leaves

This is one possible explanation. Compare the signs below with those on your plant.

What to look for

Compare window-facing and sheltered leaves. Note whether patches follow the margin, lie between veins or occur at random.

Choose a leaf with a small, separate lesion while the edge is still easy to see. Find a second leaf of similar age on a different shoot. Include a vein junction in a close photo so you can locate exactly the same area again after the leaf dries.

Possible causes and next steps

Sun injury, moisture stress and leaf diseases can each leave brown patches. Review changes in light and photograph a healthy new leaf for comparison.

How to tell similar problems apart

Compare the edge of a patch with nearby healthy tissue. One mark where a tie or another leaf rubbed suggests an injury; scattered new marks deserve closer inspection.

Map the patches against the light source. Broad papery areas restricted to newly exposed leaves suggest checking for a recent jump in sunlight. Small spots developing on sheltered leaves as well deserve closer inspection, particularly where nearby leaves touch when wet.

What to do now

Photograph the same patch with a ruler beside it. Water the soil directly so the surface can dry between waterings.

Before pruning, check the opposite side of the plant at the same height. The distribution may reveal which leaves were shaded by branches or neighboring pots. If you request identification, include the plant name and both views of an early lesion while there is still green tissue around it.

What to check next

If the patch grows or marks appear on new growth, bring photos showing the whole plant and both sides of the affected tissue.
