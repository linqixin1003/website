Checking for leaf-feeding caterpillars

The two larvae are pale green, segmented, and finely hairy. The holes beside them show that they are feeding on this leaf.

Inspect the specimen

Look at the legs at the front and the prolegs farther back. Touch one gently and note whether it curls or drops. A greener, almost hairless larva with many prolegs may be a sawfly larva and should not be added here automatically.

Check similar symptoms

The hairs in the photograph are fine and dense. A large larva with an obvious horn on the side does not belong on this page.

Choose a response

Remove larvae only after you have matched them to this leaf. An insect that appears only at night should be checked in the evening, not by treating the whole leaf surface by day.

Track the next change

Note the approximate length. If a week later only empty holes remain, this group has left.

Host-specific check

Use this page only when these larvae are on a leaf with fresh holes. An egg mass on bark is not this insect.

Photograph a larva in place before removing it, including the leaf edge or hole beside it. Check again for new pellets and biting on that shoot. When holes are present but no fresh feeding or larvae appear, avoid assigning them to the first caterpillar found elsewhere in the garden.
