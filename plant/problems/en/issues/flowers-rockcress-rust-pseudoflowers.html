Yellow leaf clusters mimic rockcress flowers

A bright yellow patch on a spring rockcress may look like a flower from several steps away. Close inspection can reveal a rosette of altered leaves produced in association with a rust fungus.

Trace the yellow blades

Start at the base of the yellow cluster. Follow individual blades to their attachment on the shoot. The flower-like display is made of modified leaves; their broad surfaces may carry many small orange-yellow structures.

Find a real flower

Look for a normal flower on another plant of the same kind. Compare its four petals and central reproductive parts with the leaf cluster. An insect visiting the yellow structure does not settle the question because the mimic can also attract visitors.

Keep the stem in view

Photograph the entire shoot and the cluster from above and from the side. A close crop showing yellow colour alone loses the information that distinguishes a pseudoflower from an ordinary yellow wildflower.

Record its setting

Note the host and whether neighbouring stems are flowering normally. On a wild plant, an observation and photographs are sufficient. An exact rust species name may require a specialist familiar with the host and the local flora.

View the yellow cluster from underneath. Blades inserted at several levels along a short stem reveal a stack of modified leaves. A normal rockcress blossom has its petals together around a single centre at the tip of a flower stalk.

Will these yellow clusters set seed like the other flowers?

First find the centre of one display. A real rockcress flower has petals around reproductive organs; after the petals fall, follow that stalk for an elongating seed pod. In a rust pseudoflower the yellow blades are leaves attached at successive points on a shoot. Tag a genuinely flowering stem separately if you want to collect seed. On wild plants, leave the unusual shoot in place for observation.
