Gray mold

Gray-brown fuzzy growth on stems

This is one possible explanation. Compare the signs below with those on your plant.

What to look for

Check for growth near wounds, spent blooms or dead tissue. Test whether the stem is still firm.

Possible causes and next steps

Gray mold can develop in damp conditions. Remove spent blooms and dead leaves and reduce crowding.

How to tell similar problems apart

Look for fuzzy growth growing directly from damaged tissue. Soil particles or a dusty deposit on an otherwise firm surface may look similar from a distance.

Start with the oldest flower or leaf touching the stem. Ease it aside without shaking it and look underneath. A collapsed patch directly below a spent blossom is easy to miss from above. Check the next two joints for trapped petals, particularly on the side facing the wall or another plant.

What to do now

Remove dead petals and damaged material lodged against the affected surface. Reduce prolonged surface wetness and leave space around crowded shoots.

Slip a bag under loose debris before lifting it away. Work when foliage is dry and clean the blades before moving to another plant. If only the fallen flower is moldy and the supporting stem is firm, leave the stem in place. Space pots so leaves no longer rest wet against each other after watering.

What to check next

If decay spreads into firm tissue or onto nearby shoots, document the growing edge before discarding damaged material.

Revisit the exact joint after the surface has dried. Watch for a dark area extending around the shoot or wilting of leaves above it. If either appears, get the damaged stem identified while the boundary with living tissue is still visible. A photo of dried debris alone offers fewer clues.
