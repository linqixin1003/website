Tent-shaped mines in apple leaves

The photograph shows one pale, slightly raised mine on the underside, beside a vein. It is not a scatter of pale spots on the upper surface.

Inspect the specimen

Turn the leaf over and see whether the pale area sits between the surfaces, tented, and whether a dark speck lies inside it.

Check similar symptoms

Pale specks on the upper surface with no raised mine underneath stay off this page. A hole that opens to the light is different from a mine that still has a skin over it.

Choose a response

Leave a few mines on the tree and pick up fallen leaves. This page does not give a spray rate.

Track the next change

If the next young leaf shows the same raised mine only on the underside, keep this page.

Host-specific check

Use this page for the underside of an apple leaf. Spots on the fruit belong with a fruit guide page.

Pick one mined leaf from a marked spur and note how many intact green leaves remain on it. On the next visit examine those remaining leaves for new mines. An existing tent becoming more obvious as it expands is a different observation from previously clean leaves becoming occupied.

If fruit quality is the concern, inspect the fruit directly beneath the mined leaf. A ripening apple can remain sound beside a conspicuous mine. A puncture in the skin leading into brown tracks in the flesh calls for a separate fruit-insect diagnosis.
