Tiny mites and fine webbing

The webbing and the tiny animals are on the underside. Pinpoint flecks with no webbing underneath start on the stippling guide page.

What to look for

Inspect the underside for fine webs and moving dots, and the upper surface for pale stippling.

Possible causes and next steps

Spider mites can leave stippled leaves. Rinse washable foliage and separate the pot for follow-up checks.

How to tell similar problems apart

Fine speckling alone does not identify mites. Look under leaves for tiny moving dots and fine webbing; an ordinary spider web can be much coarser.

Choose leaves from the most speckled part and from its edge. Tap each one separately over plain white paper so that you know which leaf produced moving specks. Observe with a hand lens and inspect the leaf underside for fine silk around the veins. Dust will fall too; movement alone does not identify the species. A single large spider web between pots is a different clue from fine webbing close against damaged leaves.

What to do now

Tap a suspect leaf over white paper and watch for movement with a lens. On plants that tolerate rinsing, wash the undersides gently.

Where the plant tolerates rinsing, support the leaf and direct water at its underside. Let the pot drain afterward. Watering an already wet root ball again will not remove mites from the canopy. Set aside one previously clean leaf as a comparison and inspect nearby plants whose foliage was touching. Fresh stippling and live mites there are more informative than waiting for old bronze leaves to recover their colour.

What to check next

Check nearby leaves for fresh stippling. Old speckles remain even after mites are controlled.

Tap one stippled leaf over white paper and watch the specks before moving the paper. Moving mites alongside fine webbing give stronger evidence than a web alone. Repeat on a green neighboring leaf after rinsing to check whether mites have reached fresh foliage.

Will old feeding speckles turn green again?

Pale stippling left by feeding can persist. Examine the underside of a previously clean neighboring leaf with a lens and tap it over white paper to check for moving specks. Judge progress by new foliage remaining clean and fewer active mites, rather than expecting the old damaged leaf to become uniformly green.
