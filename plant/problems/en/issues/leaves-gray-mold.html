Gray fuzzy growth on damaged leaves

This photograph shows a stem, not a leaf. On a leaf, gray mold is a separate check: gray fuzz on dead tissue or a spent petal, not the thick coating on this stem.

Inspect the specimen

Check a fresh brown lesion and any spent petal resting against it. Gray fuzzy growth on damaged tissue supports gray mold, especially in damp conditions; the growth may be absent when the tissue is dry.

Check similar symptoms

Powdery mildew can develop on living leaves, and affected leaves may yellow or twist. Gray mold more often forms gray tufts on browning or decaying tissue under humid conditions. Wiping either surface does not establish the diagnosis.

Choose a response

Remove flowers and leaves that carry the gray fuzz and bag them. Keep water off the foliage that remains.

Track the next change

If a new flower grows gray fuzz where it is fading, keep this page, and also look for the thick stem coating shown in the photograph.

Host-specific check

Use this page for gray fuzz on a leaf or flower. Thick mold on the middle of a stem belongs with the stem guide page.

Check beneath fallen petals

On flowering pot plants, lift a spent petal resting against a leaf and look at the tissue directly under it. A matching damp brown patch with gray growth provides more context than mold on a loose petal alone. Remove spent flowers gently without brushing growth across healthy leaves.

Inspect the dense center of the plant after the outer leaves have dried. If those inner leaves remain wet where several petioles cross, change watering position so water reaches the potting mix without collecting in that pocket.
