Bean leaf beetle damage to leaves and pods

The photograph shows the adult beetle, not leaf holes or pod scars. Check those on the bean plant.

Bean leaf beetles can injure a green bean crop twice over: tender leaves are chewed and young pods acquire shallow scars. Whether the crop is destined for whole fresh pods or shelled beans changes how much the surface injury matters.

Where to look

Examine small plants first, including the seed leaves and newly opening leaflets. Then check developing pods alongside affected foliage for roughened patches where the beetle has removed the outer surface.

What confirms it

Watch quietly for a small oval beetle before turning the leaf. The body color varies, and the usual spots may be faint or absent. A dark triangle near the base of the wing covers is helpful.

Similar damage

Foliage with holes has many possible culprits. Leaf and pod injury on the same bean plant, along with an adult showing the triangular mark, gives a stronger identification than hole size alone.

What to do

Inspect plants for adults before covering young rows with insect netting. Handpick visible beetles in a small garden and protect young growth where the proportion of leaf area lost is large.

Follow-up

Follow the next set of pods through harvest. Old pods cannot heal a surface scar; judge activity by newly formed scars and how much healthy leaf surface the plant retains.

Part the canopy and compare pods at two stages on the same plant. Scarring confined to older pods can reflect a previous visit. Freshly grazed patches on small expanding pods deserve attention even when the larger leaves still have enough green tissue to sustain the plant.
