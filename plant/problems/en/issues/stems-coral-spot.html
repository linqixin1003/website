Coral-colored cushions on dead twigs

Small pinkish cushions on a pruning stub tell you exactly where to start investigating. The important question is whether the wood carrying them was already dead or whether living spurs nearby are failing too.

Inspect the plant

Inspect shaded dead twigs and old pruning stubs for pinhead-sized salmon cushions. Check where dead bark meets a living branch. Include the point where the twig joins last season's wood in your photograph. An isolated macro photograph of the cushions leaves this relationship invisible.

Distinguish similar signs

A few orange structures on wood already dead do not explain decline throughout the canopy. Trace injured twigs toward their point of attachment.

Choose a response

Where a small lateral shoot is dead, prune it cleanly in dry weather. Avoid leaving long stubs that can support fungal growth.

Follow up

Revisit the adjacent spur when new shoots expand. If more shoots collapse, submit a section including live and dead tissue for diagnosis.

A detail to record

On an apple branch bearing coral cushions, compare buds on the affected spur with buds just below it at spring budbreak. Record the lowest bud that fails to open.

Separate dead twigs from living growth

Follow a dotted dead twig inward until you reach plump buds or leaves. Include this boundary in the photo. If the cushions remain on last year's dead wood and the current shoots extend normally, monitor the number of newly dead twigs. A boundary moving into living wood warrants checking the shrub's growing conditions and the original cause of decline.
