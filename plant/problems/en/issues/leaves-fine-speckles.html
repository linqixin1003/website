Fine brown speckling

The brown dots are small and scattered across the leaf. Dots that have merged into wide patches belong with the brown-patch guide page.

What to look for

Inspect leaf undersides with a hand lens. See whether dots wipe off and look near the veins for moving pests.

Examine the underside directly below a cluster of speckles. Note whether you see moving mites, fine webbing or only discoloured leaf tissue. Speckles alone cannot identify the pest.

Possible causes and next steps

Feeding injury, deposits and dead tissue can all look speckled. Record the pattern and underside findings before choosing a treatment.

How to tell similar problems apart

Compare the scattered brown flecks with the surrounding green blade under a hand lens. A speck that remains part of the tissue after a gentle rinse differs from loose debris sitting on top.

Look at individual pinpricks through a lens. Check for movement or fine threads underneath a speckled leaf. If present, examine adjacent foliage for mites. Speckles embedded in a dry patch with no surface activity warrant tracking the patch boundary as well.

What to do now

Note whether the dots occur mainly on older exposed leaves or also on sheltered new growth. This distribution helps narrow the possibilities when the dots are too small to identify individually.

Inspect the shaded underside in good light. Dust sitting on the blade can mask the contrast between pale feeding points and small areas of dead tissue. If you request identification, include the plant name and both views of an early lesion while there is still green tissue around it.

What to check next

If several flecks enlarge and join, photograph the transition while separate dots are still visible nearby. A fully brown patch may no longer show how the injury began.

Separate old dust marks from fresh injury

Choose one speckled leaf and one clean neighbor on the same shoot. With a hand lens, check for tiny moving mites on the undersides and for empty pale skins near veins. If the clean leaf develops speckles, inspect it promptly, before dust settles into the damaged surface.
