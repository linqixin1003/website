Dark upright eggs cling to asparagus spears

The common asparagus beetle presents two different problems over a growing season. Adults and their attached eggs can mark the spears picked for the table. Once harvest has stopped, larvae and adults may feed on the tall ferny growth that supports the perennial planting for the following year.

Where to look

Examine the spear tip and scales before harvest. Narrow dark eggs fastened on end may be easier to see than the adult common asparagus beetle. Look at several spears of different heights before picking them. A beetle on the far side may slip from view as you move around the bed, while the attached eggs stay fixed to the surface.

What confirms it

Look for a dark beetle with pale patches and reddish margins. Later, soft grayish larvae feed on the ferny summer foliage.

Similar damage

The orange spotted asparagus beetle looks different and its larvae feed mainly in berries. Check where the eggs and larvae actually occur.

What to do

Harvest edible spears frequently. Handpick beetles and larvae on small plantings, checking both sides of the stems.

Follow-up

Continue checking after harvest ends. Heavy feeding on summer foliage matters because those green shoots supply the underground crown.
