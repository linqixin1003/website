Rounded swellings replace mullein flower buds

Among the buds on a mullein spike, one or two may swell into conspicuous balls. Keeping an entire section of spike in view helps distinguish a bud gall from the plant’s normal sequence of flowers and capsules.

Compare neighbouring buds

Choose buds at roughly the same height on the spike. Affected ones stand out in width and outline. The ordinary hairs on a mullein can cover a gall too, so a fuzzy surface alone does little to identify the swelling.

Check the flower stage

Follow the spike downward to a part that has already flowered. There you can recognise normal developing seed capsules. Returning to the same bud shows whether it opens like its neighbours or remains as a rounded swelling.

Record the host plant species

Photograph leaves and an open flower as well as the gall. The midge Asphondylia verbasci is recorded from buds of particular Verbascum hosts. A bulge on a different plant needs its own identification.

Check the flowering spike later

Count the unopened galls and the flowers that develop normally around them. In a wildlife planting, leave a few marked buds undisturbed and photograph their later appearance rather than removing the whole flowering stem.

Trace the rounded swelling to the flower stalk. A normal capsule usually sits where an open flower was observed earlier. A tagged unopened bud that becomes round while neighbours release yellow petals gives a clearer sequence of evidence.
