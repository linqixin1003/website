Soft, rotting flowers

The petals are soft and wet, not merely dry. A brown bloom that is still papery belongs with the dry-flower guide page.

What to look for

Inspect overlapping petals for water-soaked tissue. Compare newly opened flowers with older blooms.

Possible causes and next steps

Petals damaged while wet can decay quickly. Remove collapsed blooms, allow air between flowers and water the soil below them.

How to tell similar problems apart

Press lightly beside the damaged area. Soft collapse can follow a bruise, chilling or decay; spreading softness is more concerning than a stable dent. Direct irrigation toward the potting mix or soil so the remaining flowers can dry.

Look between overlapping petals first. After rain, water sometimes remains trapped there even after the outer petals dry. Note whether the soft part is confined to a fold or continues into the green cup below the bloom. Check neighboring flowers where their petals touch, especially in a dense cluster.

What to do now

Lift fallen petals off the leaves beneath the bloom. If the flower collapses when handled, remove it gently so that damp fragments do not remain wedged in the next bud.

A crushed petal may show a single crease where the bloom rubbed against a support. Several water-soaked brown patches that join together, especially with gray fuzz, warrant removing the affected bloom. Clip it in dry conditions and lift it straight out so the petals do not fall onto healthy flowers beneath.

What to check next

Check the green tissue immediately behind the petals. Note whether it stays firm after the flower fades or whether softness extends through the flower base into the stalk.

Look again after the next damp night. Check buds that were tightly closed at the first inspection and the leaves directly below spent flowers. A fallen petal stuck to a leaf can conceal a new patch of damaged tissue.

Check where petals touch after rain. If a wet, collapsed petal sticks to the flower below, lift away the spent flower and inspect the contact point on the lower one. Leave enough space for adjacent blooms to dry before wrapping or bringing them indoors.

Should I remove a whole flower cluster when just one bloom goes soft?

Trace the soft tissue back from the petals to the little stalk that carries that bloom. If the stalk and shared stem are firm, remove the collapsed flower without dragging it across its neighbours. If the shared stem is also soft, take off the affected cluster at a sound point with clean tools. At the next opening, inspect the youngest petals and the place where discarded wet petals used to rest.
