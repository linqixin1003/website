Identifying striped cucumber beetles

The photograph shows one adult beetle on white fabric, not a chewed seedling and not a crowd inside a blossom. Three dark stripes run along the yellow wing covers.

Inspect the specimen

Match field insects to this beetle: an elongated body, a yellow ground color, and three dark stripes reaching the wing tips. Record feeding only when the same stripes are on a cucurbit leaf or inside a cucurbit blossom.

Check similar symptoms

Spotted cucumber beetles carry spots rather than three long stripes. A round hole with no beetle in sight that day stays off this page.

Choose a response

On a small seedling with holes, remove the striped beetle you can see and note which seedling it was. Do not treat a whole row for a crowd you have not found.

Track the next change

Look again after flowering by parting the petals. Striped beetles in the blossom and holes on the young leaves are separate notes.

Host-specific check

Use this page for cucumber, squash, and their relatives. On any other crop, count the stripes before using the same name.

Before removing a seedling cover, count the healthy leaves on a few marked plants. Check those same plants once beetles can reach them. Loss of most of a seed leaf on a tiny transplant warrants quicker attention than a few bites scattered over a vigorous flowering vine.
