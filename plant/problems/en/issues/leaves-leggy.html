Pale, stretched shoots

The yellow tissue in the photograph is the seed leaves, not the old leaves of a mature specimen. The white stems are thin and long, which points to light around the time of emergence.

Inspect the specimen

See whether the yellow parts are the lowest pair of seed leaves and whether any true leaf has appeared. Yellow seed leaves plus a stretched stem are recorded as a light problem.

Check similar symptoms

A mature crown with many large leaves turning yellow on a short, thick stem does not match this seedling photograph. Use a yellowing guide page for expanded leaves.

Choose a response

Add light before deciding on fertilizer. Yellow seed leaves do not need to be picked off.

Track the next change

When the first true leaves expand, check their color. Green true leaves and shorter joints mean those seed leaves are not a fertilizer case.

Host-specific check

Keep this page for sown seedlings. Yellow leaves on an older pot specimen belong with the matching leaf guide page.

Measure the next two internodes

Mark the uppermost fully formed node before gradually increasing light. Measure the distance between the next few new nodes on the same shoot. Old stretched sections will keep their length; shorter new internodes with normally expanded leaves give a better measure of the plant’s response.

Compare shoots on the window side and room side before rotating the pot. Growth leaning toward one window, with longer gaps on shaded shoots, supports uneven light. If all shoots of this cultivar have always produced long trailing stems, compare with an earlier photograph before pruning.
