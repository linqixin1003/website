Leaf beetles and fresh chewing damage

The clear subject is the beetle on a green leaf, with stripes along the wing covers. Chew holes are not what this photograph demonstrates.

Inspect the specimen

Photograph the same striped wing covers first, then look for a fresh notch on a neighboring leaf. If the notch and the beetle are on different leaves, photograph them separately.

Check similar symptoms

A weevil has a snout that projects. This beetle's head does not. Holes with no beetle on the foliage stay off this page.

Choose a response

Remove beetles when they are few. If holes are numerous and no beetle appears for several days, look again in the evening instead of stripping the leaves by day.

Track the next change

Check the leaf underside in the evening. If new holes appear and the beetle is still absent, record the holes on their own.

Host-specific check

Use this page when this striped beetle is on a leaf. A beetle inside a flower belongs with the flower guide page.

Connect the beetle to fresh feeding

Mark one intact leaf beside the feeding site and revisit it during the beetles’ active period. Watch which insect actually bites the edge. A resting adult on an old hole, especially where several insect species share the plant, is poor evidence for treating the whole planting.

On seedlings, compare the newest true leaf with the cotyledons. Record whether fresh holes are accumulating as quickly as new leaves expand. An established plant and a tiny seedling with the same number of holes have very different amounts of remaining foliage.
