Rust-colored spots and pustules

The reddish marks carry powdery pustules. A brown spot with a yellow edge and no powder belongs with the yellow-halo guide page.

What to look for

Check the underside for orange-brown pustules and the corresponding upper surface for pale spots.

Examine the surface under light coming from the side. Raised material casts a small shadow, while a flat change in the leaf itself usually does not. Look at the reverse of the same patch and at the leaf directly above it before deciding where the material came from.

Possible causes and next steps

Rust disease is one possibility. Avoid handling wet leaves and monitor new growth for similar pustules.

How to tell similar problems apart

Look under a spot for a raised pustule. Some rusts release coloured dust; a flat orange stain alone is insufficient for identification.

Match a pale spot on the upper surface with the same location underneath. A raised orange or brown pustule there is a more useful clue than the color alone. Handle a suspect leaf gently and wash hands before touching healthy foliage to avoid transferring powder.

What to do now

Photograph both leaf surfaces before handling. Keep heavily affected fallen leaves away from healthy foliage.

Compare several intact pustules before disturbing them. A stain directly below a damaged pustule may be loose powder that fell onto the next leaf. Keep a photo of the original surface so you can distinguish a new deposit from one missed during cleaning.

What to check next

If spots recur on new leaves, note the plant species and season, which help distinguish different rust diseases.

Look for fresh pustules of more than one age

Old rust pustules can darken and may release little powder. Compare them with a newly developed spot on the same host, recording which side bears the raised structure. A flat orange stain exactly beneath a rusty hook or support calls for checking that contact point as well.
