A miniature pineapple at the base of a spruce shoot

Follow the shoot past the swelling. The position of a spruce gall helps distinguish two common adelgid injuries.

What to check first

On a spruce, inspect the point where the season's new shoot begins. The eastern spruce gall adelgid produces a compact pineapple-like swelling in this region. Green spring galls are easy to overlook among the needles; older ones turn brown and stand out.

How to tell similar problems apart

Check the shoot tip as well. A more elongated gall at the very end of the shoot fits Cooley spruce gall better. An ordinary seed cone hangs separately from the shoot; a gall incorporates the shoot tissue. Include the branch beyond the swelling in your photograph.

What to do now

On a large tree with just a few old brown galls, note their positions and leave the healthy crown intact. If a small spruce has several distorted leaders, document which leader is affected before pruning. Removing the growing tip can change the shape of the tree.

What to check next

Look at new shoots next spring. Count fresh green galls separately from last year's brown structures. Repeated formation on a young tree or breakage at the same swollen joints is more informative than the number of old galls visible in a single photograph.
