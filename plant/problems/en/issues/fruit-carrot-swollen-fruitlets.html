Individual fruits swell in a carrot-family seed head

A few inflated fruitlets can stand out on an otherwise normal carrot-family umbel. Count the individual swellings and identify the plant before naming the gall.

What to look for

Inspect an umbel after flowering. Among many small developing fruits, a few may form conspicuously enlarged rounded galls, sometimes pink or purplish. Look at each little stalk separately.

Check the pattern

Check that the swelling replaces an individual fruit. A cluster of aphids sits on the surface, while this structure is continuous with the plant tissue. Host identification helps narrow down the gall midge involved.

What to do

Photograph the side and top of the umbel together with a leaf. If the plant is wild, leave the head intact for observation. Do not identify a carrot-family plant for eating from its gall.

When to check again

Compare neighbouring umbels as seeds ripen. Record how many fruitlets are swollen on each head and whether normal fruit continues to mature beside them. This distinguishes a few local galls from whole-head failure.

Count fruitlets rather than flower heads

Hold a normal and an affected umbel side by side at the same stage after bloom. A swelling at the end of just one small stalk has a different distribution from a thickened main stalk that carries an entire group of fruits.

Examine fruitlets at the edge and in the centre of the same umbel. Development can be uneven as the flowers set fruit in succession. Look for a discrete, enlarged fruit on its own stalk rather than a whole group merely remaining greener for longer.
