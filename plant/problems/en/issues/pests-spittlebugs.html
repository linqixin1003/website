White foam lodges in the forks of soft shoots

Meadow spittlebug nymphs surround themselves with a wet mass of bubbles while feeding on stems. The white patch can look dramatic against a dark green plant. Opening a single fresh patch usually provides more information about the cause than examining a large area of dried marks.

Where to look

Search grass blades, herb stems and leaf axils for patches of moist white froth. Note whether the plant is otherwise growing normally. Take a close photograph before washing the shoot. Individual air bubbles and the position of the shelter where the stem meets a leaf will be easier to see in a fresh mass than in one that has collapsed.

What confirms it

Part one fresh mass carefully and look for a small soft-bodied nymph nestled against the stem. The froth shelters the feeding insect.

Similar damage

Mealybug wax looks cottony rather than bubbly. Powdery mildew forms a coating on the leaf and has no insect sitting inside a mass of wet bubbles.

What to do

On vigorous garden plants a few froth patches usually require little attention. If they are objectionable, wash them away and dislodge the nymphs.

Follow-up

Recheck tender growth for fresh foam rather than counting dried residue. Persistent wilting merits a separate check of roots and water supply.

Wash one foam-covered stem and leave a neighboring occupied stem for comparison. Reappearance of wet froth at a leaf joint makes that joint worth reopening for a nymph. A pale dry stain at the former site is not evidence that another nymph has moved back.
