Raised white blisters underneath brassica leaves

White eruptions in a mustard-family crop can push through the leaf skin. Seeing an intact blister alongside one that has already split helps distinguish this pattern from a loose dusting across the leaf.

Inspect the plant

Turn a leaf over where the upper surface has a pale yellow patch. White rust forms discrete chalk-white blisters that bulge through the lower surface. Check emerging flower stalks when the crop is bolting. A photo that includes affected leaves and distorted reproductive growth shows whether the symptoms extend beyond a single leaf.

Distinguish similar signs

Older pustules rupture and expose powder. Downy mildew tends to produce fuzzy growth in damp conditions, so inspect the texture before assigning a name.

Choose a response

Examine stems and flower heads for distortion. Remove affected crop residues and susceptible volunteer plants, and limit prolonged overhead irrigation.

Follow up

Mark plants around the first patch and inspect their leaf undersides after cool wet weather. New intact pustules make better diagnostic material than dry powder alone.

A detail to record

On a mustard plant with a distorted flower stalk, follow that stalk down to the nearest fully expanded leaves and inspect their undersides. Record whether the same individual carries both blisters and distorted growth.

Choose an intact blister for the sample

Cut a whole affected leaf with its stalk, leaving at least one unopened pustule untouched. A loose spoonful of powder loses the link between the white material and the leaf epidermis. Note the crop type, since pale patches on an unrelated neighboring weed may have a different cause.
