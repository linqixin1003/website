White dashes coat wintercreeper stems and yellow-spotted leaves

Euonymus scale is an armored scale insect that can spread unnoticed through a dense evergreen planting. The conspicuous white marks and the less obvious brown shell-shaped scales are different forms of the same pest. Check both foliage and sheltered shoots.

Where to look

Part the canopy of wintercreeper or evergreen euonymus and examine leaves from one yellowing shoot. Use a lens to inspect the midrib, lower leaf surface and adjoining bark.

What confirms it

Look for slender white male covers and larger brown female covers shaped like miniature oyster shells. Heavy colonies can build a crust over leaves and twigs, and affected branches may lose leaves.

Similar damage

Powdery mildew makes a superficial powdery film. Here the individual covers have defined edges and occur in different shapes, often beside small yellow feeding spots in the leaf.

What to do

Remove badly encrusted branches when the infestation is localized. Check protected interior stems before deciding how far the problem extends; the showiest outer leaves can miss much of the colony.

Follow-up

Revisit clean new leaves and examine twigs for tiny moving crawlers during local hatch periods. Old covers can remain attached after insects die, so their persistence alone is an unreliable measure of activity.
