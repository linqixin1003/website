Rose sawfly feeding damage

The photograph shows leaf tissue scraped thin between the veins, and the veins stay green. Pale larvae are not the subject of this frame.

Inspect the specimen

Hold a leaflet to the light. The thin area should lie between veins, with the skin still present. Turn the leaf and photograph any larva on its own rather than treating this feeding photo as proof of a larva.

Check similar symptoms

A dark dead blotch does not leave the vein network translucent. A hole is missing tissue, unlike this window-like scrape.

Choose a response

While larvae are still on the foliage, remove the leaflet being scraped. If only a dry old patch remains and no larva is present, do not cut the shrub back hard.

Track the next change

When a new leaflet shows a fresh translucent patch, check the underside again. An old dry patch can remain on a mature leaflet.

Host-specific check

Keep this page for roses. Check the leaf shape before adding a translucent patch from another shrub.

Inspect a leaflet that has one transparent patch and a broad green margin. Look where the two meet before removing anything. A larva still scraping that boundary can be picked off individually; a dry patch surrounded by sound new growth adds little reason to prune a flowering stem.
