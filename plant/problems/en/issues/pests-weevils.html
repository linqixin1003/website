Leaf weevils

Snouted beetles and notched leaf edges

This is one possible explanation. Compare the signs below with those on your plant.

What to look for

Check for semicircular notches and inspect plants after dusk for snouted beetles.

Possible causes and next steps

Adult weevils can notch leaves. If the whole plant wilts too, inspect the roots for damage.

How to tell similar problems apart

Repeated notches along a leaf edge suggest chewing. A snout on the adult helps distinguish weevils from other beetles.

Examine a fresh notch from above and from the side. Repeated bites cut into an otherwise intact margin can fit adult weevil feeding; a hole that began as a dead spot in the centre of a leaf is different. In daylight, search the sheltered side of the pot and debris nearby. At night, check the same damaged shoot for a beetle with a projecting snout and photograph it beside the bite.

What to do now

Check the plant and rim of its pot after dark with a torch. Photograph any beetle beside a fresh notch.

Treat damage above and below the soil as separate observations. A healthy established shrub can tolerate some notching. In a container, persistent wilt in moist soil warrants checking drainage and the outer roots if the root ball slips out easily. Photograph any pale grub beside damaged roots before moving it. Many insects have pale larvae, and a root problem may have another cause even when an adult weevil has visited the leaves.

What to check next

If a potted plant also wilts despite moist soil, inspect the root ball for damage before assuming the leaf notches explain the wilt.
