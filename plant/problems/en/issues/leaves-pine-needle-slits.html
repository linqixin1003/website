Elongated black slits on fallen pine needles

A hand lens can reveal small black structures on pine litter well after the needles have fallen. Interpreting them requires a second piece of evidence from the branch that shed those needles.

Inspect the plant

Examine fallen needles with a hand lens and find the oval dark structures aligned with their length. In damp conditions a narrow slit may open along the middle. Separate needles picked up under mixed conifers by host whenever possible. A mixed bag of litter can contain several fungi and may not correspond to the tree being investigated.

Distinguish similar signs

Some Lophodermium species colonize dead needles. Black structures on litter alone cannot establish why a living branch lost its foliage.

Choose a response

Compare needle ages on the affected branch and on a neighboring pine. Improve spacing where foliage stays wet, and avoid directing irrigation into the canopy.

Follow up

Collect recently discolored attached needles together with fallen ones for diagnosis. Record when shedding started relative to the spring flush.

A detail to record

Match litter to a pine by comparing intact needle bundles with those still attached overhead. If the litter includes both short paired needles and longer bundles of three, label the samples separately.

Check fallen needles against living branches

Place a still-attached needle from the affected branch beside a fallen one and photograph both with the same magnification. If dark slits appear only on long-dead litter while successive needle cohorts remain intact on the tree, the litter finding alone does not explain decline in another pine.
