Identifying longhorn beetles near trees

The photograph is the beetle on stone. There is no bark and no exit hole. The antennae are longer than the body and banded pale and dark.

Inspect the specimen

Banded antennae and pale spots on the wing covers warrant a closer look. Other longhorned beetles can resemble the Asian longhorned beetle; these features alone do not establish the species.

Check similar symptoms

On a nearby hardwood, a round exit hole or fresh shavings at the base may be useful even if the adult has gone. Several wood-boring insects leave similar signs.

Choose a response

If you suspect Asian longhorned beetle, photograph it and record the location. Leave nearby firewood, branches and logs in place. In the United States, report the sighting to USDA APHIS or your state plant health office; elsewhere, contact your local plant health authority.

Track the next change

A specialist can assess the insect or tree damage before you decide on pruning or removal. Include any photos you can take from the ground.

Host-specific check

Report what you actually saw: a suspect adult, a hole or wood debris. You can report a sighting even without a photograph of all three.

Photograph the beetle and its exact resting place separately. An adult sitting on a wall or stone does not identify an infested tree. If fresh wood debris repeatedly collects beneath one opening on a nearby stem, photograph that opening and host for a local identification before cutting wood.
