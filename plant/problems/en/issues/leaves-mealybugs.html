Mealybugs in leaf axils

The photograph shows only the insect, not a leaf axil. On a leaf, look in the angle where the stalk meets the blade for the same oval wax-covered insect.

Inspect the specimen

Part the axil and look under the wax for the oval body and the rear filaments, then check the midrib on the underside. Do not stop after a glance across the upper surface.

Check similar symptoms

A continuous white film with no insect underneath is checked as powdery mildew. Fibers and mineral crusts have no segmented body.

Choose a response

Move the pot aside. Where the leaf is sturdy, lift a small group from the axil with a damp swab.

Track the next change

Mark one axil you have cleared and look there again a week later for a new insect.

Host-specific check

Use this page for the axil and the underside. An insect under a dry sheath on the stem belongs with the stem guide page.

Inspect points where leaves meet

Pull adjacent leaves gently apart and look into petiole axils for waxy clusters, small crawlers and sticky deposits. A lone tuft on a blade may be plant fiber or packing material. Inspect new growth and the pot rim before moving the plant back among others.

Watch a patch of white wax with a lens before wiping it away. An oval segmented body moving beneath wax filaments supports mealybugs. Cottony masses attached to immobile scale covers may be egg sacs; keep a close photograph if the distinction is unclear.
