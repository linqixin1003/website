A spring silk tent fills the fork of a cherry branch

Eastern tent caterpillars share a silk shelter in a branch fork and leave it to feed on nearby foliage. Inspect the host tree and season along with the tent. The location of feeding relative to the silk helps distinguish a spring colony from a different group of web-building caterpillars.

Where to look

Check the crotches of cherry and ornamental crabapple branches as the trees leaf out. A dense silk tent can persist between the branches while nearby foliage disappears. Look along the branches leading away from the shelter. A nest that appears empty during one inspection may still be in use if caterpillars are feeding farther out; check again when the group has gathered.

What confirms it

Observe whether hairy striped caterpillars travel out of the tent to feed. The nest serves as a gathering place and does not have to enclose the leaves being eaten.

Similar damage

Fall webworms enclose foliage at branch tips later in the growing season. The position of the silk relative to a fork or a leaf cluster is especially useful.

What to do

Remove a small reachable tent together with its occupants when they are gathered inside. Keep flames away from bark and live branches.

Follow-up

Inspect nearby branch forks for additional colonies. Judge the tree’s recovery by subsequent leaf growth, particularly on a young or repeatedly defoliated tree.

Check the limb where the tent is attached before removing it. If caterpillars are away feeding on foliage, taking down the empty silk misses the colony. Return when they have gathered at the shelter and inspect the neighboring fork for another occupied tent.
