White powdery coating on fruit

The white film wipes off and the skin underneath is still there. A brown sunken mark that will not wipe off belongs with the spotting guide page.

What to look for

Inspect the distribution and nearby leaves. Compare the normal waxy bloom of the cultivar with newly appearing irregular patches.

Possible causes and next steps

Some fungal diseases also leave powdery patches. Avoid rubbing fruit skins and track spread to leaves or new fruit.

How to tell similar problems apart

Natural wax often coats similar surfaces evenly. Powdery growth more often forms irregular patches that enlarge or appear on fresh tissue. Check several berries of the same variety and maturity before deciding that their usual surface has changed.

On grapes and blueberries, examine an untouched fruit beside one you have handled. A finger-shaped dark streak in an otherwise even haze can be where natural surface wax rubbed away. Look beneath the fruit as well as on top. A coating that follows the exposed side in dried droplets may instead reflect a recent spray.

What to do now

Compare affected and healthy surfaces on the same plant. Photograph the coating before touching it, and leave healthy fruit wax intact.

If the patches look uneven, inspect the youngest leaves, stems and other fruit on that shoot. On grapes, powdery mildew on young berries can interfere with skin growth, and the skins may later split as the berries expand. Photograph the berry skin and the accompanying leaf symptoms before disturbing the coating.

What to check next

If new patches spread to shoots or leaves, identify the host and seek advice on whether treatment is needed.

Leave one cluster unhandled and compare it again as berries enlarge. A uniform coating on intact fruit and expanding patches accompanied by scarring or cracking suggest different causes. Rubbing the surface alone cannot settle the distinction.

Hold two berries side by side before rubbing the surface. Natural waxy bloom is easily lost where fingers or leaves touch it; smooth finger-shaped gaps can therefore be handling marks. Look for cracking, uneven growth or leaf symptoms before blaming a white coating on disease.
