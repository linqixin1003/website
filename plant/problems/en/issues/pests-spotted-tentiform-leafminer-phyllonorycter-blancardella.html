Tent-shaped feeding tunnels made by larvae in apple leaves

The pale patch beside the vein shows where a larva fed inside this apple leaf. Seen from below, the surface puckers into a small tent.

Inspect the specimen

Turn the leaf over and look along the veins for a pale, raised patch. A dark speck may be visible beneath the thin outer layer.

Check similar symptoms

Hold the leaf up to the light. A feeding tunnel still has a thin covering of leaf tissue. Light passes directly through a hole chewed all the way through the leaf.

Choose a response

If only a few leaves show feeding damage, leave them on the tree and monitor them. Gather fallen leaves at the end of the season.

Track the next change

Check nearby leaves for new pale, tent-shaped patches, especially along the veins on the underside.

Host-specific check

Look for these tunnels in apple leaves. Inspect any blemishes on the fruit separately.

Mark a short fruiting branch and photograph an affected leaf in place. At the next visit, compare the original patch with the photograph and check previously clear leaves for fresh feeding damage.

Inspect the apple below an affected leaf. The fruit may have no insect damage. A puncture in the peel with a brown tunnel in the flesh suggests a separate fruit pest.
