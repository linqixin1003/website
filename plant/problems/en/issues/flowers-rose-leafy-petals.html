Green leaflets replace parts of a rose

When parts inside a normally coloured rose take the shape of leaves, inspect the next flush before deciding whether the change is persistent.

What to look for

Separate the petals gently and look for little green blades in positions usually occupied by floral parts. Photograph an ordinary bloom of the same cultivar beside it if possible.

Check the pattern

Check whether that cultivar normally has green flowers. A green outer sepal is also ordinary. The useful clue is a change of form inside a flower that usually produces normal petals and reproductive parts.

What to do

Note heat or other stress around the time buds formed. Rose phyllody can be physiological. A single affected bloom with healthy shoots gives little reason to assume an infectious problem.

When to check again

Inspect flowers in the next flush. If leafy flowers persist across multiple canes or other parts of the plant change, show a diagnostic service both normal and affected growth before propagating from the plant.

Compare equivalent flowers

Choose another flower at the same stage of opening. Young petals may still show their green bases; a distinctly leaf-shaped blade in the flower centre is a better clue. Record whether similar blades occur on several blooms opening during the same week.
