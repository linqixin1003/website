Silvery patches on stored potato skins

A smooth patch that flashes silver when a potato is wet can point to silver scurf. The reflective appearance comes from changes in the skin, so the angle of the light matters.

Inspect the specimen

Wet one sample tuber and slowly rotate it under a lamp. Photograph the outline of the patch while wet and again once dry. The symptom is particularly easy to see on red-skinned potatoes.

Check similar symptoms

A superficial abrasion typically has a visible scraped edge. Black scurf has raised dark bodies; common scab has a corky texture. A soft, foul-smelling patch needs investigation for another storage rot.

Choose a response

Separate affected lots and check whether condensation forms on tubers or containers. Follow local potato storage guidance on temperature and ventilation; repeated wetting encourages the disease during storage.

Track the next change

Inspect the same lot again for enlarging patches and shriveling. Note whether marks were already present at harvest; lesions that were difficult to see then may become conspicuous after time in storage.

Host-specific check

In red potatoes, compare the suspect patch with equally wet, unblemished skin under the same light. General shine from wetting the whole tuber can otherwise mask the patch outline.
