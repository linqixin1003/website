Dark, horn-like bodies replace individual grass seeds

A few hard, dark projections may stand out among the pale grains of a mature grass head. When these structures take the place of individual seeds, consider ergot and keep the material separate from food or feed.

Locate the dark structure

Look along one intact head. A suspect body emerges from the position normally occupied by a grain and may extend beyond the surrounding husk. Photograph it in place with a normal seed nearby for scale.

Compare it with smut

An ergot body is a discrete solid structure. Loose smut can reduce most of a head to exposed dark powder and leave a bare central axis. A dark awn is part of the flower bract and is usually far thinner.

Protect the harvest

Keep suspect grain, seed heads and hay away from livestock and household food supplies. If the material is intended for feeding or milling, ask a qualified agricultural laboratory or extension adviser how to have the lot assessed.

Check beyond one head

Record the grass species if known and photograph heads in several parts of the patch. A close-up can document the appearance, but the colour or size of one ergot body cannot establish the safety of a batch.
