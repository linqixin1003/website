A felted sleeve traps a grass flower head

Watch a grass clump as it comes into head. On an affected shoot, a tightly fitting white or yellow-orange sleeve can surround the upper sheath where the flower head normally pushes through.

Look at the attachment

Follow the sleeve around the stem without peeling it off. In grass choke the growth encircles the sheath, often for a noticeable length. The trapped head may fail to extend while heads on neighbouring tillers emerge freely.

Separate bands from pustules

Scattered orange leaf-rust pustules make many separate marks on a blade. A choked shoot has a continuous sheath-like band. In older affected shoots the originally pale band can become yellow, so colour alone is a poor guide.

Identify the grass

Save a photograph of a normal head beside an affected tiller and include the leaf blades. Related fungi can cause similar sleeves on different grass hosts. If seed production matters, ask a local grass specialist to confirm the host and diagnosis.

Follow individual tillers

Mark the affected tiller, then check whether it produces a seed head when others have finished emerging. Count affected shoots separately from affected clumps; one clump can carry both normally flowering and choked tillers.

Compare shoots only after the normal heads on that patch have emerged. A late tiller can still have a smooth, green sheath around an unopened head. The felted sleeve on a choked tiller remains attached as surrounding stems lengthen.
