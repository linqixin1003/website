Mealybugs

Cottony insects in leaf joints

This is one possible explanation. Compare the signs below with those on your plant.

What to look for

Look into leaf axils for wax-coated insects and sticky honeydew.

Inspect the plant before washing or moving it. Start where the damage looks freshest and turn the leaf over without shaking the shoot. Include the attachment to the stem in your inspection. A hand lens or a close phone photo can reveal detail that a picture of the whole plant misses.

Possible causes and next steps

These signs suggest mealybugs. Isolate the plant, wipe off small groups and recheck new growth.

How to tell similar problems apart

Wax-covered insects collect in leaf joints. Fuzzy deposits without a segmented insect underneath may have another cause.

Part the leaf joints gently and inspect the white material for an oval, segmented body beneath waxy threads. Also check pot rims and contacting plants. If the white material is a continuous flat coating across the blade, examine the surface closely before treating for insects.

What to do now

Separate the pot from adjoining foliage and inspect sheltered joints and pot rims. Lift small colonies with a damp swab.

Check the underside of ties and labels if insects keep reappearing at the same joint. These sheltered spaces can conceal individuals during a quick surface inspection. Save a close-up of any occupant alongside a picture showing the injury on the plant.

What to check next

Reinspect previously clean shoots. New cottony clusters there matter more than wax left at an old feeding site.
