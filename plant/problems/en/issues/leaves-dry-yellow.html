Lower leaves yellow and wilt in dry soil

Feel the soil first. It is dry, and yellowing with limpness starts on the lower leaves. The same yellowing in wet soil belongs with the wet-soil guide page.

The photo illustrates the visible yellowing. Check soil moisture to distinguish possible causes.

What to look for

Check the middle of the root ball; water can run down the sides without wetting the center.

Compare the weight of the pot with its weight after a normal watering. An unusually light pot and dry mix below the surface help distinguish a dry root ball from yellowing caused by poor drainage.

Possible causes and next steps

If the root ball is dry, water slowly until moistened, then drain. Watch for recovery in younger leaves.

How to tell similar problems apart

The surface of a pot can be dry while its centre is wet. Judge moisture at root depth and lift the pot before deciding to water.

Notice whether water runs down a gap beside a shrunken root ball and exits immediately. In that case apply water slowly in stages, then check whether the center actually moistened. If it is already damp inside, investigate root condition before giving another soak.

What to do now

If the root ball is dry, moisten it evenly and let excess drain. If it is wet, remove standing water and check the outlet.

When lifting a newly watered pot, notice whether it has actually gained weight. Rapid drainage from a nearly weightless pot deserves another check of the center. At the next inspection, compare the moisture at root depth with your first reading.

What to check next

Look for recovery of firmness and healthy new growth. If the plant wilts in wet soil, inspect the root ball rather than watering again.

Check for water bypassing a dry root ball

In a pot that has dried severely, water may run down a gap along the pot wall. After watering, check the center of the root ball as well as the edge. If only the rim is moist, rewet the mix gradually and let excess water drain before judging the wilt.
