Sunken bark with concentric ridges

Each ridge marks an attempt by the tree to grow around damaged bark. The number, arrangement and condition of those ridges are more informative than bark color viewed from a distance.

Inspect the plant

Look where a branch or pruning stub joins older wood. Repeated growth around a sunken dead patch can leave a series of target-like raised edges. Take one picture straight on and another along the branch. The second view shows whether the wound occupies one face or has almost encircled the limb.

Distinguish similar signs

A single wound can also develop a raised rim. Look for successive ridges and tiny red fruiting bodies before attributing the injury to European canker.

Choose a response

On small branches, choose dry conditions to prune into healthy wood. Keep tools clean and avoid creating additional bark wounds around the cut.

Follow up

Photograph the outermost ridge with a scale. Further dieback beyond it or involvement of a major scaffold warrants specialist advice.

A detail to record

On an apple spur, locate the flower or fruit stalk scar relative to the canker. Record whether a sunken band extends onto the supporting branch or ends on the short spur.

When the lesion sits at a major fork

Ridges can surround a strip of living bark, and counting them will not tell you the strength of a branch. Note whether the lesion lies at a load-bearing fork and how much crown extends beyond it. A cracked main fork above a path deserves an arborist’s assessment even if the leaves on that limb are still green.
