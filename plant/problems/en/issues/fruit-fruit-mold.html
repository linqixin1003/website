Mold growing on fruit

Visible mold on a soft berry can extend below the skin. In a picked container, discard the whole moldy berry and those that touched it. Inspect the rest and wash sound berries before eating.

What to look for

Inspect tissue beneath the mold, crowding between fruit and bruised areas.

Possible causes and next steps

Decaying fruit can affect neighbors. Remove affected fruit, improve airflow and inspect the next crop.

How to tell similar problems apart

Look for fuzzy growth growing directly from damaged tissue. Soil particles or a dusty deposit on an otherwise firm surface may look similar from a distance. When picking, check the underside before placing each berry in the container with the sound fruit.

Turn the cluster gently to see the side hidden from view. On strawberries, start under the leafy cap and where two berries press together. A small patch can soften in that sheltered spot before gray growth appears on the visible side. Check the matching contact point on the neighboring berry as well.

What to do now

Remove dead petals and damaged material lodged against the affected surface. Reduce prolonged surface wetness and leave space around crowded shoots.

Lift the whole moldy fruit into a separate container without brushing it through the cluster. Pick ripe, sound berries separately and avoid piling them deeply enough to bruise the bottom layer. If low fruit regularly rests against wet ground, clean straw beneath the cluster can help keep it off the soil.

What to check next

If decay spreads into firm tissue or onto neighbouring fruit, document the growing edge before discarding damaged material.

Return as the next green berries begin to color. A strawberry can acquire gray mold infection during bloom and show decay only as it ripens. Record where the first soft areas appear, even when the flowers looked healthy earlier.

Lift the strawberry by its stalk and check the hidden side resting against mulch. Separate sound fruit before picking the mouldy one, and use a different container for discards; a soft berry can smear neighbouring fruit during harvest.
