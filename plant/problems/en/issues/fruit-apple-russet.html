Dry bronze netting spreads over apple skin

Rough bronze skin can be characteristic of an apple variety or appear after conditions affecting the young fruit. The texture, distribution and cultivar all matter.

What to look for

Examine the shoulder and stem cavity of the apple. Russeting appears as bronze or brown roughened skin, sometimes spreading in a fine network across an otherwise well-shaped fruit.

Check the pattern

Rub the surface gently. A coating of dust moves away, whereas russet is part of the skin. Discrete olive scab lesions, especially with similar spots on leaves, merit checking for apple scab.

What to do

Check the cultivar description. Some apples naturally develop extensive russet. In normally smooth varieties, exposure to wet conditions or spray injury when fruit was young may help explain a particular season’s pattern.

When to check again

Compare fruit from sheltered and exposed branches at harvest, and repeat the comparison next year. Record any changes in spray timing alongside weather notes before attributing the rough skin to one cause.

Compare the flesh below the skin

Cut across the edge of a roughened area. If the underlying flesh stays firm and normally coloured, record a skin change. A soft expanding brown zone or deep corky pits are additional symptoms and should be assessed independently.

For a naturally russeted cultivar, compare equivalent areas on several apples, including the stem cavity and lower cheek. An even pattern repeated on sound fruit is a better baseline than a picture of a different, smooth-skinned variety.
