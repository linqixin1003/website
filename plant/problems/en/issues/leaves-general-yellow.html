Nutrient deficiency

Widespread leaf yellowing

This is one possible explanation. Compare the signs below with those on your plant.

What to look for

Record whether yellowing began on young or old leaves and whether exposure is uniform.

Look along a single growing shoot from the oldest attached leaf to the unfolding tip. Note where the change first becomes visible. Compare it with another shoot receiving similar light. A photo of the whole shoot preserves this sequence better than a close-up of the palest leaf alone.

Possible causes and next steps

Water, light and nutrition all influence color. Check recent changes and root condition before feeding.

How to tell similar problems apart

Yellowing throughout the plant can follow root stress as well as a nutrient shortage. Record whether old or new leaves changed first.

Notice whether the whole plant is pale or whether lower shaded leaves account for most of the change. Before feeding a uniformly yellow plant, inspect the drainage and recent fertilizer history. Adding fertilizer to a stressed, wet root system can complicate recovery.

What to do now

Check drainage and the recent feeding history before adding fertilizer. For garden beds, a soil test can guide the choice of amendment.

Include one normally colored leaf in the frame when possible. Yellow indoor lighting can make it difficult to compare a new photo with an earlier one. If further leaves change color, keep the product name and amount of the most recent fertilizer with the photos.

What to check next

Judge recovery on the next leaves. Rapid yellowing with wilting or root decay warrants a closer root inspection.
