Recognizing scale insects on leaves

Look for small fixed bumps beside the veins or on the underside of a leaf. The photo shows a single tan cover. A hand lens can help distinguish an occupied cover from one left behind after an insect has died.

Inspect the specimen

With a hand lens, examine the edge of a bump. On an armored scale, a cover may lift to reveal the insect below. Soft scales have no separate removable cover, so do not force a firmly attached bump off a tender leaf.

Check similar symptoms

A mealybug can move and often has wax filaments at the rear. This cover is fixed to the leaf and has no long filaments.

Choose a response

When covers are few, wipe them with a damp cloth, after trying one small area of the leaf so you can see whether the surface tears.

Track the next change

Check previously clean young leaves for newly settled scales. Empty covers can remain on older leaves after the insects die.

Host-specific check

Use leaf veins and the lower leaf surface as your inspection points. If the bumps are chiefly on woody bark, consult the stem scale guide.

Examine several covers near a leaf vein. Where a cover lifts easily, look underneath for an insect or eggs. If the old covers are empty, use a lens on neighboring leaves to look for tiny moving crawlers before deciding whether the infestation is still active.
