Tulip leaves show pale blotches and a bloom collapses in the clump

Look at the whole clump before picking a single leaf. In the photograph the pale blotches are on the leaves, and a discolored flower has collapsed at their base.

Inspect the specimen

The pale areas have uneven edges and can run together into a bleached patch. The flower is not held up on its stalk; it is crumpled among the leaves. Record both on the same plant.

Check similar symptoms

Gray fuzz only on petals that have been open for a long time, with evenly green leaves, fits spent flowers instead. Holes chewed from a leaf while the flower stays intact are a different record.

Choose a response

Lift plants with a collapsed flower, bulb included, and bag them away from the bed. Do not bury those leaves and flowers back among the tulips.

Track the next change

When leaves emerge next year, look first for the same pale blotches. Damage limited to an old leaf tip, with the flower stalk still upright, should be noted separately.

Host-specific check

Compare tulips in the same bed that have not collapsed. If the blotches stay on one tulip variety while neighboring bulbs keep even leaves, write down the variety and the year it was planted.

Look into the fold where two petals overlapped before the flower opened. A mark confined to the outer, weather-exposed face has a different distribution from flecks on several petals and spotting on the leaves below. Keep the cultivar label with the photographs.
