Fruit spotting

Dark spots on fruit

This is one possible explanation. Compare the signs below with those on your plant.

What to look for

Compare spot placement, sunken areas and other fruit on the same plant.

Possible causes and next steps

Abrasion, sun injury and infection may darken fruit. Track spot enlargement and avoid bruising healthy fruit.

How to tell similar problems apart

Look at the skin in side light. A shallow healed scrape, a sunken enlarging lesion and many tiny punctures suggest different problems even when all appear brown. For very small marks, include a wider picture showing their distribution across the whole fruit.

Turn the fruit without rubbing the skin. Check whether the marks face the sun, line up with a twig that brushes the fruit, or appear equally on sheltered surfaces. A row of shallow scrapes at twig height suggests friction. Similar spots scattered across fruits that never touch a branch merit a closer look.

What to do now

Photograph an early spot beside a ruler. Compare exposed and shaded fruit and cut one damaged fruit to see whether discoloration extends into the flesh.

On one removed fruit, slice through the center of a spot and compare it with unaffected flesh on the opposite side. Record whether the mark ends at the skin or sits above a pocket of brown tissue. Clean the knife afterward. Leave a second spotted fruit intact for follow-up if it is still firm.

What to check next

Check the same fruit and neighboring young fruits after wet weather. Rapid expansion, softening or similar spots on leaves makes a local diagnostic review more useful.

Use the stalk or another fixed feature to locate the same mark in subsequent photos. An enlarging rim or newly sunken center is easier to recognize when both pictures show the same side at the same scale.
