Individual capsules swell bright yellow in aspen catkins

On a fruiting catkin of an aspen or grey poplar, look for swollen yellow capsules among normal ones. The change may affect a few individual fruits or transform much of the hanging catkin.

Look along the axis

Follow one catkin from its stalk to its tip. Compare successive capsules and note whether the swollen bodies occupy the same positions as normal fruits. Keeping the axis in the photograph makes the origin of these galls much easier to recognise.

Check which catkin you have

Look for female fruiting structures. A male catkin carrying pollen has a different job and appearance. Compare with a normal fruiting catkin on the same tree so differences between sexes or stages do not confuse the comparison.

Look back at the tree

Record leaf shape, the tree's identity if known, and the date. A fallen yellow fragment tells far less than a catkin still attached to a recognised host. Grey poplar and aspen are both recorded hosts.

Track fruit development

Revisit marked branches as sound capsules mature. Note whether swelling stays restricted to catkins, and record any separate leaf or twig damage independently. There is no reason to prune a mature tree solely to photograph a few affected fruits.

Wait until nearby capsules start releasing cottony seeds. Normal seed fluff sits outside a splitting capsule and blows away; a yellow gall was an enlarged part of the capsule itself. Note the stage when making comparisons across different poplar trees.
