Gray patches with dark rims

The patch is gray with a darker rim. A plain brown patch without that rim belongs with the brown-patch guide page.

What to look for

Compare the pale center and dark margin, noting any spots merging together.

Find the smallest spot that still has a gray centre and an intact rim. Compare its rim with the pale tissue inside. An older spot whose centre has torn away gives you less information about where the damage began.

Possible causes and next steps

Several leaf disorders form contrasting margins. Reduce water splash and photograph changes for identification.

How to tell similar problems apart

A dark rim around a pale centre can occur with several leaf spots. Check whether the centre is sunken and how the spot follows the veins.

Examine the dark rim where gray tissue meets green. A dry gray center alone is hard to identify. Lesions extending along a vein or onto a leaf stalk deserve close photos of both areas. Also inspect nearby twigs before deciding the damage is confined to blades.

What to do now

Avoid handling wet foliage. Examine stems and fruit for similar sunken marks before removing badly damaged leaves.

Check more than the largest patch. Older centers may tear or bleach, hiding the early edge and giving the same lesion a very different appearance. If you request identification, include the plant name and both views of an early lesion while there is still green tissue around it.

What to check next

If young shoots or fruit also develop lesions, include those parts in the photographs you submit for identification.

Locate the edge that is still changing

A worn gray center may crack or fall out long after the original injury. At the next inspection, look for a new narrow band of discolored tissue beyond the old dark rim. Include that transition and adjacent healthy green tissue in a sample, rather than collecting only the dry center.
