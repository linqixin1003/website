Tiny black pinpoints cluster on apple skin

A smooth apple can develop groups of small black dots that look like pepper sprinkled over the peel. Look at their spacing and the condition of the skin underneath before interpreting them as insect damage.

Inspect a group closely

Use a hand lens and a side light. In flyspeck, numerous separate dark pinpoints are grouped on the surface. A dirty grey or olive wash with less distinct edges suggests sooty blotch, which can occur on the same fruit.

Check the peel below

Cut one marked apple through a group of dots. Firm, normally coloured flesh beneath an intact peel fits a superficial blemish. A hole with crumbs, a deep pit or soft brown flesh requires a separate explanation.

Look inside the canopy

Compare shaded inner branches with exposed outer ones. A dense canopy that stays wet for long periods can favour these surface fungi. During ordinary pruning, maintain enough space for light and air around developing fruit.

Review at harvest

Set aside several representative apples before polishing them. Photograph the range of markings. If counts change from year to year, record late-season wet weather as well as the cultivars and branches affected.

Choose two apples on one branch, one tucked against foliage and the other hanging clear. Examine the shaded cheek as well as the outward-facing side. Tiny groups missed on a quick walk past may be easy to see when the fruit is turned.
