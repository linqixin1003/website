Brown spots with yellow halos

A yellow ring surrounds the brown spot. A brown patch with no yellow ring belongs with the brown-patch guide page, and a spot with powdery pustules belongs with the rust guide page.

What to look for

Look for a yellow ring around each spot and check whether the border appears water-soaked.

Choose a leaf with a small, separate lesion while the edge is still easy to see. Find a second leaf of similar age on a different shoot. Include a vein junction in a close photo so you can locate exactly the same area again after the leaf dries.

Possible causes and next steps

Some leaf spot diseases produce halos. Water at soil level, keep wet leaves apart and watch for additional spots.

How to tell similar problems apart

A yellow halo can surround several kinds of injury or infection. Note whether the spot crosses the veins or stops at them.

Look at the green edge outside the yellow ring. If the ring and brown center enlarge together on several leaves, document a young lesion before the halos merge. If a single ring surrounds a puncture that stays small, check for thorns, rubbing or recent handling damage.

What to do now

Clear fallen affected leaves and avoid splashing water onto healthy foliage. Photograph a young spot before its centre dries out.

Include some unaffected blade around the halo in the picture. This gives a reference for the real leaf color when comparing photos taken under different lighting. If you request identification, include the plant name and both views of an early lesion while there is still green tissue around it.

What to check next

If halos appear on previously clean leaves, note recent rain or overhead watering and ask for diagnosis before choosing a spray.

Track the halo and dead center separately

Photograph a young spot with a ruler and identify its brown center and its yellow margin. At the next check, measure the center separately. A yellow zone can widen as the surrounding leaf ages, so measuring only the outer halo may exaggerate expansion of dead tissue.

Does a yellow halo mean this is bacterial?

A halo shows that leaf tissue around the lesion has changed; it cannot identify the cause on its own. Hold a recently spotted leaf to the light and check for a water-soaked appearance or margins bounded by veins. For identification, select a leaf with an early lesion and living surrounding tissue rather than submitting only a completely dry leaf.
