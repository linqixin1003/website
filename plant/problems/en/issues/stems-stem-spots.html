Brown spot

Brown spots on stems

This is one possible explanation. Compare the signs below with those on your plant.

What to look for

Photograph the edge of a spot. Check whether the surface is flat, corky, sunken or cracked.

Possible causes and next steps

Normal corking, abrasions and lesions can look similar. Mark a spot and watch for expansion onto younger growth.

How to tell similar problems apart

Compare the edge of a patch with nearby healthy tissue. One mark where a tie or another leaf rubbed suggests an injury; scattered new marks deserve closer inspection.

Inspect both sides of the stem in daylight. Is the mark flat, sunken or visibly raised? Does it repeat at every leaf joint? Compare with an uninjured stem of similar age. Regular pores or leaf attachment scars can be conspicuous on older growth, especially when younger shoots are still smooth and green.

What to do now

Photograph the same patch with a ruler beside it. Water the soil directly so the surface can dry between waterings.

Trace the outline in a photo and include a nearby bud for orientation. If spots occur only on the side touching a stake, move the contact point and inspect the protected side for comparison. Where several spots occur without obvious injury, keep wet stems apart and clean cutters between plants. Do not shave a spot off to inspect it.

What to check next

If the patch grows or marks appear on new growth, bring photos showing the whole plant and both sides of the affected tissue.

Check whether an edge crosses a recognizable landmark, such as the base of a bud. A patch reaching around the stem together with drooping foliage above it is a reason to seek identification promptly. Include photos taken before and after any support was moved.
