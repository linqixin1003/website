Brown spot

Fine brown speckling

This is one possible explanation. Compare the signs below with those on your plant.

What to look for

Inspect leaf undersides with a hand lens. See whether dots wipe off and look near the veins for moving pests.

Choose a leaf with a small, separate lesion while the edge is still easy to see. Find a second leaf of similar age on a different shoot. Include a vein junction in a close photo so you can locate exactly the same area again after the leaf dries.

Possible causes and next steps

Feeding injury, deposits and dead tissue can all look speckled. Record the pattern and underside findings before choosing a treatment.

How to tell similar problems apart

Look at the specks with a lens. Surface particles may lift off; dead spots embedded in the blade remain when wiped gently.

Look at individual pinpricks through a lens. Check for movement or fine threads underneath a speckled leaf. If present, examine adjacent foliage for mites. Speckles embedded in a dry patch with no surface activity warrant tracking the patch boundary as well.

What to do now

Test a small area with a damp white cloth and compare both sides. Examine nearby leaves before deciding how widespread the problem is.

Inspect the shaded underside in good light. Dust sitting on the blade can mask the contrast between pale feeding points and small areas of dead tissue. If you request identification, include the plant name and both views of an early lesion while there is still green tissue around it.

What to check next

If dots merge into dead patches or spread to new leaves, keep an intact sample for closer examination.
