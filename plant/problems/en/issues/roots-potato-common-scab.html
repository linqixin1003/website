Corky pits or raised scabs in potato skin

The lesions of common scab are part of the potato skin itself. They may be shallow and rough, distinctly raised, or pitted, so several tubers from one hill need not look identical.

Inspect the specimen

Wash a sample and let excess water drain. Feel the transition between smooth skin and corky patches. Cut across one lesion on a sample tuber to record how far the damaged tissue extends.

Check similar symptoms

Black scurf leaves dark deposits on top of the skin. Powdery scab can produce open pits with ragged edges. If the distinction matters for a seed lot, keep whole uncut specimens for a clinic.

Choose a response

Record how dry the bed was when young tubers were forming. For a future crop, use clean seed potatoes and a locally recommended, less susceptible cultivar, and keep moisture even during tuber initiation.

Track the next change

Compare the proportion affected in each bed at the next harvest. Save the cultivar name and soil-test result alongside the photographs before deciding whether a soil amendment is appropriate.

Host-specific check

Compare the rough patch with an ordinary eye on the same tuber. Buds and leaf scars occur at eyes, while corky scab can extend across the spaces between them.

Cut through the deepest corky pit on one tuber. Record whether rough tissue ends close to the skin or penetrates in a pocket. If the interior is wet and breaking down, keep that tuber apart and assess the soft decay separately from the dry scab.
