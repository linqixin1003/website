Club-shaped roots on wilting brassicas

A wilting cabbage with damp soil under it deserves a root check before another watering. A distorted root system can leave the plant short of water even when its bed is moist.

Inspect the plant

Lift one affected brassica with the roots intact. Look for spindle-shaped enlargements or a knobbly club replacing much of the normal branching root system. When comparing plants, keep the soil from each root sample in its own container. Returning mixed soil to a clean bed could spread a problem that persists in the ground.

Distinguish similar signs

A plant may wilt on a sunny afternoon and recover overnight. Compare roots with a healthy plant: temporary wilting by itself also occurs in dry soil.

Choose a response

Keep contaminated soil on site. Remove infected plants and clean soil off tools and boots before moving into a clean bed.

Follow up

Record where cabbage relatives and susceptible weeds grew. Plan a long rotation away from brassicas and consult local guidance before adjusting soil pH.

A detail to record

On a turnip or radish, distinguish the naturally swollen edible organ from the fine roots below it. Irregular swellings extending onto those side roots are more informative than the size of the harvested bulb.

On cabbage or broccoli, compare plants at the edge of a wilting patch with their healthy neighbours in the cool morning. Afternoon flagging may recover, but clubbed roots remain enlarged overnight. Lift one small symptomatic plant with its root shape intact if examination is necessary.
