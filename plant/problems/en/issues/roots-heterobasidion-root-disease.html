Pale-edged conks low on conifer stumps and roots

A cluster of declining conifers near an old stump calls for a look at ground level. Heterobasidion root disease can involve roots well before the canopy provides a clear diagnostic pattern.

Inspect the specimen

Inspect naturally exposed roots and protected hollows near stump bases for irregular brackets. The upper surface can be brown while the pore surface beneath and the growing margin appear pale. Photograph the attachment point.

Check similar symptoms

A bracket on buried dead wood does not prove that a neighboring live tree is infected. Look for a pattern of decline around stumps and record the conifer species, spacing and previous thinning or removal.

Choose a response

If a tree with root decay could fall onto a building or path, arrange a qualified tree-risk inspection. Do not excavate major roots to look for more conks or pull on the tree to test its stability.

Track the next change

Mark the outer edge of the group and return to the same viewpoint to track new decline. Supply the stump locations as well as the photographs when seeking a local forest-health diagnosis.

Host-specific check

On slopes, map each stump uphill or downhill of the affected group. A site sketch preserves their actual positions better than one tightly cropped picture of a conk.

Look for an accessible old stump in the same group of conifers. Record a conk on a stump separately from symptoms on living trees; finding a fruiting body on the stump does not establish how much sound wood remains in its standing neighbour.
