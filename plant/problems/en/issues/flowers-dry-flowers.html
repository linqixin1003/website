Dry, brown flowers

The petals are dry and brown, not yet soft and wet. A bloom that collapses into mush belongs with the soft-flower guide page.

What to look for

Is browning confined to older blooms? Check unopened buds and the leaves on the same stem.

Possible causes and next steps

Older blooms dry as they age. If unopened buds also dry, inspect the root ball for drought and check for hot air blowing on the plant.

How to tell similar problems apart

Older petals often dry after a bloom has opened normally. Compare them with unopened buds: papery buds that never open suggest stress before flowering. Feel a fallen petal between your fingers and note whether it crumbles or sticks together.

Follow a single flowering stem from its oldest blossom to its smallest bud. On a hydrangea, a whole mature flower head can become papery while the leaves beneath it stay green. A fresh bud that goes crisp on the side facing hot glass tells a different story, especially if shaded buds still open.

What to do now

Check moisture in the root zone and see whether only flowers facing a heater, hot window or drying wind are affected. Remove spent blooms if seed is not wanted.

For a potted plant, feel the potting mix partway down before reaching for the watering can. If it is already moist and the damaged flowers all face a vent, move the pot out of the air stream first. Watering again will do little to protect those petals from hot air.

What to check next

Follow a newly formed bud through opening. If fresh buds repeatedly fail while soil moisture remains appropriate, inspect inside a damaged bud for insects or discolored tissue.

Write down which buds were still green when you moved the plant. Judge the change by those blooms. If browning begins as damp spots or reaches down the flower stalk, inspect for decay before adjusting watering again.

Look at the last green tissue beneath a dry flower. If the stalk stays firm and the next bud opens normally, remove the spent bloom at its usual deadheading point. If several still-closed buds crisp on the same side, check exposure to hot glass or moving air there.

The flowers are dropping. Should I water more to keep the rest?

Pick up one freshly fallen flower and look at the plant it came from. If only the petals have fallen and a firm, green ovary remains on the stalk, follow that ovary as it develops; extra water cannot restore spent petals. If unopened buds are dropping too, check the root ball before watering. Rehydrate genuinely dry mix and let it drain. If the mix is already moist, inspect the bud stalks and recent temperature changes instead.
