Yellow veins in green tissue

The veins are yellow and the tissue between them stays green. Yellow tissue between green veins belongs with the interveinal guide page.

What to look for

Compare several young and old leaves. Note whether color follows the veins and whether shape is changing.

Look along a single growing shoot from the oldest attached leaf to the unfolding tip. Note where the change first becomes visible. Compare it with another shoot receiving similar light. A photo of the whole shoot preserves this sequence better than a close-up of the palest leaf alone.

Possible causes and next steps

The cause of vein discoloration depends on the host. Document progression and seek identification if more leaves develop it.

How to tell similar problems apart

Compare the veins themselves with the tissue between them, especially on the newest leaves. The two yellowing patterns can point in different directions.

Hold a fully expanded leaf flat in diffuse daylight and trace one main vein into its smaller branches. Note whether these lines are actually yellow or merely look pale against darker tissue. If the pattern repeats alongside distortion, keep propagation material from that plant separate pending identification.

What to do now

Use natural light and compare leaves of the same age. Review root condition and soil pH before adding individual nutrients.

Check whether yellow lines are visible from both surfaces. Take one picture against a neutral background because backlighting can exaggerate naturally translucent veins. If further leaves change color, keep the product name and amount of the most recent fertilizer with the photos.

What to check next

If the pattern persists in new growth, keep photos of young and mature leaves and arrange a host-specific soil or tissue assessment.

Verify that the veins themselves are yellow

View the leaf against a plain dark background in diffuse light. Trace a yellow line from the midrib into a side vein, then compare it with a normally colored leaf of the same variety. Backlighting alone can make healthy veins look bright; persistent yellow veins in new growth deserve closer examination.
