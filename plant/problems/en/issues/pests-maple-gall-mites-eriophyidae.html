Small red or green bladders rise from maple leaves

Bladder gall mites stimulate localized growth on expanding maple leaves. The little swellings consist of leaf tissue, so they remain attached as the leaf matures. Several striking colors can occur on the same tree without indicating several different diseases.

Where to look

Look most closely at the upper surface of a silver or red maple leaf with beadlike bumps. Turn it over and match the position of each swelling to the underside.

What confirms it

The mites are too small for reliable unaided identification. The gall’s shape, position and host provide better field clues. A small opening on the lower surface communicates with the gall interior.

Similar damage

Other maple gall mites produce slender spindle-shaped projections instead. A scale insect sits on top of the leaf; a gall is continuous with the leaf and does not lift away as a separate cover.

What to do

On an established healthy maple the bumps are usually cosmetic. Leave functioning leaves in place. Spraying an already formed gall is unlikely to remove it or reach the mites protected inside.

Follow-up

Photograph the tree as well as a close-up leaf and compare the next flush of growth. Broad canopy decline calls for examination beyond the conspicuous galls on individual leaves.
