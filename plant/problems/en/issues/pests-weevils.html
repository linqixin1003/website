Identifying weevils on garden plants

Use the photograph to recognize the beetle: dark, with a clear snout, resting on a grass stem. It is not chewing a leaf.

Inspect the specimen

Look at the length of the snout and the pale scales on the wing covers. Match it to a beetle found on a leaf, and do not use this page if the snout differs.

Check similar symptoms

A leaf beetle has no snout this long and a rounder body. The snout is the feature that matters here.

Choose a response

Remove the beetle only after the snout and the color match. Do not treat a neighboring broadleaf specimen because a beetle was resting on grass.

Track the next change

If two evenings in a row show no beetle with the same snout on that specimen, stop attributing notches to it.

Host-specific check

Use this page to recognize the beetle. The leaf guide page is for the notch, and the notch has to be visible.

Inspect a marked, undamaged leaf margin after dark and again the next morning. If a new notch appeared, search the same plant and the pot rim before disturbing the foliage. A snouted beetle found farther away helps identify a visitor, but it does not connect that individual to the bite.

A beetle that drops when the plant is touched is easy to lose in mulch. Hold a pale tray beneath a notched branch before inspecting it after dusk. Keep the catch associated with that particular branch; beetles swept from mixed ground cover give much weaker evidence about the host.
