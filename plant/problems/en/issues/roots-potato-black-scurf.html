Black crusts that cling to potato skin

A harvested potato can look dirty even after a careful rinse. Inspect individual black crusts on the skin and compare them with the loose soil that washes away from the eyes.

Inspect the specimen

Black scurf consists of irregular dark survival structures on the surface. On a tuber set aside for inspection, gently dislodge one with a fingernail and check whether intact skin lies beneath.

Check similar symptoms

Corky tissue forming part of the skin points more toward common scab. A cavity or a soft wet region extending through the flesh calls for a separate diagnosis, even when black scurf is also present.

Choose a response

At harvest, check stems just below the soil line for sunken brown areas and compare tubers from weak and vigorous hills. The stem and tuber symptoms can look quite different.

Track the next change

Keep affected tubers out of planting stock. Label the bed and seed lot separately; finding black crusts in one harvest cannot determine by itself whether the original source was soil or seed potatoes.

Host-specific check

Check the stolon attachment and the rest of the skin separately. Record the number of crusted tubers in the hill, including those with only a few small flecks.
