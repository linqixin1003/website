Scale insects

Shell-like bumps on stems or leaves

This is one possible explanation. Compare the signs below with those on your plant.

What to look for

Inspect bumps along veins and stems with a lens and distinguish surface shells from the plant tissue.

Possible causes and next steps

Scale insects can settle on stems and leaves. Separate the affected pot and inspect neighboring plants.

How to tell similar problems apart

Inspect several bumps with a hand lens. Regularly spaced pores in bark are normal; a scale insect has a distinct outline on the surface.

Compare the suspect bumps on a young shoot with those on an older stem. On bark, small corky pores often repeat in a pattern and are continuous with the surface. Examine a bump at its edge under good light. Stop if lifting it starts to tear the stem; damage to the bark will obscure the original sign.

What to do now

Inspect leaf veins, petioles and woody joints. Gently lift one suspect scale on a sturdy stem rather than scraping the entire plant.

Check leaves below the suspect colony for sticky drops and dark surface growth. Some scales produce honeydew, while armored scales do not, so a clean leaf does not settle the question. For an isolated group on a sturdy plant, remove visible insects gently and record the joint you cleaned. Avoid scraping tender new shoots.

What to check next

Empty covers may remain attached. Check for new insects on untreated growth before repeating any treatment.

Inspect the clean joint and adjacent growth with a lens for tiny moving young insects. If fresh colonies form across several stems, get the scale identified before selecting treatment. The stage present, the species of plant and whether it grows indoors all affect the choice.
