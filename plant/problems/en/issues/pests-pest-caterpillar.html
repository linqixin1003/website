Checking for leaf-feeding caterpillars

Two finely hairy larvae rest beside holes in a leaf. Fresh feeding, droppings or a larva seen chewing would strengthen the link between the insect and the damage.

Inspect the specimen

Photograph the larva where it rests. A close side view may show the legs and abdominal prolegs. Avoid touching an unfamiliar hairy or spiny larva; hairs on some species can irritate skin.

Check similar symptoms

The hairs in the photograph are fine and dense. A large larva with an obvious horn on the side does not belong on this page.

Choose a response

Check whether fresh feeding continues before deciding on removal. If damage is limited and the plant is growing well, keep the leaf and monitor it.

Track the next change

Note the date and the leaf you inspected. Old holes remain after feeding stops; new damage is a better reason to inspect the shoot again.

Host-specific check

Use this page only when these larvae are on a leaf with fresh holes. An egg mass on bark is not this insect.

Photograph a larva in place before removing it, including the leaf edge or hole beside it. Check again for new pellets and biting on that shoot. When holes are present but no fresh feeding or larvae appear, avoid assigning them to the first caterpillar found elsewhere in the garden.
