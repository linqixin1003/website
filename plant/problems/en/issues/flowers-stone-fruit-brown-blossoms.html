Blossoms on a fruiting twig turn brown and stay attached

A row of browned flowers is still joined to this short twig by their stalks. A few leaves at the tip are also brown and crumpled, while the canopy behind them is green.

Inspect the specimen

Count the dead flowers along the spur and see whether their stalks are still firmly attached to the wood. Then look at the leaves on that same twig: the whole spur may be brown, or only the flowers and a few neighboring leaves.

Check similar symptoms

Tan velvet cushions on a peach are the fruit-stage record, and this photograph shows no fruit. Petals that merely dry at the edges after a long bloom, then drop on their own, are not blossoms that remain hanging.

Choose a response

If the whole spur is dead, cut it back to healthy wood and bag it rather than leaving it under the tree. Remove any dried old fruit still hanging. This page does not give a spray rate.

Track the next change

At the next bloom, see whether newly browned flowers stay on a few spurs or spread onto larger branches. Count only brown flowers still attached. Fallen petals are a separate note.

Host-specific check

Record hanging brown blossoms on the flowering spurs of a fruit tree. A soft herbaceous flower belongs with the soft-flower guide page.

Trace a cluster of dead blossoms to its fruiting spur. Check the bark just below it for a sunken area or gum, then look for living buds farther along the twig. This distinguishes injury confined to flowers from a twig that is also dying back.
