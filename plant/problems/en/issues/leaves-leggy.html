Insufficient light

Pale, stretched shoots

This is one possible explanation. Compare the signs below with those on your plant.

What to look for

Compare the distance between nodes on new and old shoots. Check for leaning toward light.

Choose two shoots of similar age, one facing the light and one farther inside the canopy. Follow each from the growing tip back to fully expanded leaves. Record where healthy growth gives way to the change, and whether that point corresponds to a move, a hot spell or a change in care.

Possible causes and next steps

Low light can cause elongated growth. Improve light gradually within the species’ tolerance.

How to tell similar problems apart

Compare the spacing between successive leaves with older growth on the same shoot. Long gaps and a lean toward a window suggest light is limiting.

Measure the distance between two neighboring nodes on the newest firm stem and compare it with older sturdy growth. A shaded shoot reaching toward a window may benefit from a gradual increase in light. A naturally climbing shoot may simply need a support suited to its growth habit.

What to do now

Move the plant closer to suitable light gradually. Support weak stems while new growth develops.

Use a fixed landmark such as a stem branch to locate the first node produced after moving the plant. Compare only the growth beyond that landmark. Compare fully expanded leaves of similar age so the normal folds and pale color of an opening bud do not obscure the change.

What to check next

Old internodes will not shorten. Compare the spacing and firmness of the next several leaves.
