Scale insects on leaves

The photograph is one tan cover on a leaf, not a whole branch. On foliage, look for the same attached, translucent cover, especially along a vein.

Inspect the specimen

Check both surfaces. The rim is even and the color differs from the leaf. If a gentle push moves it, then see whether the spot underneath is empty or still occupied.

Check similar symptoms

A leaf spot sits inside the tissue, often with a yellow edge, and will not push off. This cover is added on top of the surface.

Choose a response

Remove only covers that move, and leave sound leaves. Do not strip every leaf for a single cover.

Track the next change

If a newly expanded leaf carries the same cover, the colony is still increasing.

Host-specific check

Use this page for either leaf surface. A cover on a twig belongs with the stem guide page.

Determine whether the cover shelters a live insect

With a hand lens, inspect several covers on a sample leaf. A dry empty cover may persist after the insect has died or emerged, while newly settled young stages can be nearly invisible. Check the midrib and petiole for smaller individuals before judging whether numbers are increasing.

Check the leaf beneath an infested branch for sticky honeydew. Soft scales commonly produce it, whereas armored scales generally do not. The absence of stickiness therefore does not clear a plant with firmly attached covers of several sizes.
