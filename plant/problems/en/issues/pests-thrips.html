Slender insects on buds and leaves

The insects are slender and mostly on flowers and young leaves. Tap a flower over white paper before deciding. A pear-shaped crowd on a soft shoot belongs with the aphid guide page.

What to look for

Tap a bloom gently over white paper and inspect slender insects with a lens. Look for scarring on petals.

Possible causes and next steps

Thrips can shelter inside buds. Separate visibly infested pots and check newly opened flowers.

How to tell similar problems apart

Silvery streaks, dark specks and slender moving insects together are stronger evidence than flower discoloration alone.

Examine a newly opened flower beside an older one. Look in the folds at the base of the petals, then check the leaf below the flower for silvery scratch-like marks and dark specks. Tap over white paper and watch for narrow insects moving away. The streak may have formed when a petal was still folded inside the bud, so the insect will not always be beside the visible scar.

What to do now

Tap a flower over white paper. Examine the insects with a lens and look between petals before discarding spent flowers.

Note whether several new flowers on the same plant show similar damage. Petals rubbing against a window or a neighbouring stem can develop scars in a consistent contact position. If moving that obstruction prevents new marks, the earlier scars may be mechanical. If live thrips and new damage continue, check nearby flowering plants as well. Keep a close-up of the insects; distorted petals alone are not enough to choose a treatment.

What to check next

Check the next buds as they open. Scars on petals already damaged will remain.

Tap a newly opened flower over a light sheet and inspect the fallen slender insects with a lens. Then examine a bud opening on the same shoot. Scars exposed as petals unfold can reflect feeding inside the closed bud; they do not prove that adults are still feeding on the open flower.
