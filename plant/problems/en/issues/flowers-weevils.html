Snouted beetles on buds and drooping flower buds

On strawberries, a clipped unopened bud may dangle from its stalk or fall beside the plant. Record that sign even if the beetle has moved on. A beetle visiting an open flower gives little evidence about who clipped the bud.

What to look for

Look just below a drooping unopened bud. Check whether the short stalk is partly cut and whether a small beetle with a snout is present on nearby buds.

Possible causes and next steps

Some blossom weevils damage unopened buds. On strawberries, compare affected bud stalks with upright buds on the same plant. Frost or rough handling can also injure buds.

How to tell similar problems apart

A bud hanging by a partly cut stalk is different from a petal with a hole. Look at unopened buds as well as open flowers. Use a hand lens to look for punctures too small to see at arm's length.

Pick up a freshly fallen bud and examine its short length of stalk. Then find the matching stub on the flower cluster. On strawberries, a punctured unopened bud together with a partly severed stalk fits the feeding and egg-laying pattern of a bud weevil. An open blossom with a dark center after frost calls for a different check.

What to do now

On strawberries, inspect the bent section just below each damaged bud and look for a small snout beetle. Photograph the attachment before removing fallen buds.

Examine several clusters along the edge of the planting and a few farther inside. Keep an intact bud beside the damaged one for scale when taking a photo. One snapped stem in a spot where a hose dragged across the plant is weak evidence for an insect problem.

What to check next

Count newly clipped buds on the next flower cluster. If losses continue, ask a local fruit adviser to identify the weevil and suitable controls.

Note how many unopened buds remain on the clusters you checked. Revisit those clusters as they open and record fresh cuts separately from empty stalks left from the first visit. That distinction matters when estimating ongoing losses.

Look beneath the damaged flower cluster for dropped buds. Compare the short stalk on one dropped bud with a partly severed stalk still on the plant. Uniform shedding of intact flowers after bloom follows a different sequence from clipping of unopened buds.
