Mealybugs

Cottony insects in leaf joints

This is one possible explanation. Compare the signs below with those on your plant.

What to look for

Look into leaf axils for wax-coated insects and sticky honeydew.

Possible causes and next steps

These signs suggest mealybugs. Isolate the plant, wipe off small groups and recheck new growth.

How to tell similar problems apart

Wax-covered insects collect in leaf joints. Fuzzy deposits without a segmented insect underneath may have another cause.

Use a light to inspect the shaded side of the stem where each leaf stalk meets it. Look beneath loose, already dead leaf sheaths without peeling away living tissue. With a magnifier, check for an oval body and short waxy filaments at its edges. Loose potting fibers can lodge in the same crevice.

What to do now

Separate the pot from adjoining foliage and inspect sheltered joints and pot rims. Lift small colonies with a damp swab.

For a few exposed insects, steady the shoot with one hand and lift the insects with a damp cotton swab. Replace the swab as it picks up wax. Check the stems of plants that touched this one, including the sides facing away from you. Keep the pots apart while you repeat these inspections.

What to check next

Reinspect previously clean shoots. New cottony clusters there matter more than wax left at an old feeding site.

Choose a clean leaf joint as a comparison point and revisit it each week. Fresh insects in several joints despite repeated removal suggest hidden colonies. If considering a treatment, check its label for both the host plant and indoor use, and test a small area for injury first.
