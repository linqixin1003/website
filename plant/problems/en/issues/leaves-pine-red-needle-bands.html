Red bands across pine needles

The tip beyond a reddish band dies while the needle base can remain green.

What to check first

Choose a low branch you can reach from the ground. Lay several partly green needles on white paper in the same direction. Look for narrow reddish or brown bands crossing the needles, with the part towards the tip becoming tan. Tiny dark structures may interrupt a band, but are not always conspicuous.

How to tell similar problems apart

Regular autumn shedding usually affects older inner needles that discolour more evenly. Winter exposure often gives a browner outer face to the plant. To compare these patterns, photograph the whole pine, the lower canopy and a needle close-up, and identify the pine species if possible.

What to do now

Dead tips cannot turn green. If a sprinkler regularly wets the lower branches, redirect it towards the root zone. When planting additional pines, allow room for their mature crowns. Avoid stripping all older needles from a thin tree simply because some bear bands.

What to check next

Mark the affected branch in a photo and watch whether new bands appear on previously green needles. Several years of substantial needle loss can weaken a tree. If the lower canopy continues thinning, request a diagnosis using symptomatic needles with intact green and discoloured sections.

Locate living tissue below the red band

Trace the needle from its sheath toward its tip. Record whether the portion below the band stays green while the tip beyond it is dead. Compare several needle bundles rather than treating a single reddened tip as diagnostic; photograph one entire bundle with the sheath intact.
