Wilting flowers

Wilting flowers

This is one possible explanation. Compare the signs below with those on your plant.

What to look for

Check the flower stalk, the moisture deeper in the pot and whether the foliage is drooping too.

Possible causes and next steps

Heat, dry soil and damaged roots can all cause drooping blooms. Check the mix before watering again.

How to tell similar problems apart

Compare the plant early in the day and during afternoon heat. Also check whether the root ball is dry, wet, or wet only around its edges. Note whether drooping begins before sunlight reaches the plant or only after several hours of exposure.

Trace the drooping flower down to its stalk. If the stalk is bent at one point but nearby leaves and buds are upright, look for a crease from handling or wind. If several stems droop together, lift the pot and inspect the drainage holes and saucer before changing the watering schedule.

What to do now

If the root ball is dry, rewet it evenly; if already wet, let it drain. Keep recently transplanted plants sheltered from drying wind while checking roots.

Dry potting mix can pull away from the sides, letting water run straight through the gap. After watering a dry pot slowly, check the center of the root ball to see if it actually became moist. Empty any water left in the saucer so the base can drain freely.

What to check next

Recovery overnight suggests temporary demand for water exceeded supply. Persistent collapse of one shoot calls for inspection of that shoot’s base.

Compare the same flowers and leaves the following morning. An old flower may remain limp even if the young foliage recovers. If the youngest leaves still sag while the mix is damp, look for damage at the crown or roots before adding more water.
