Leaf beetles

Oval beetles and chewed leaves

This is one possible explanation. Compare the signs below with those on your plant.

What to look for

Photograph the wing covers, body shape and the actual feeding site.

Inspect the plant before washing or moving it. Start where the damage looks freshest and turn the leaf over without shaking the shoot. Include the attachment to the stem in your inspection. A hand lens or a close phone photo can reveal detail that a picture of the whole plant misses.

Possible causes and next steps

Some beetles are predators. Confirm the host and observe feeding before deciding whether control is needed.

How to tell similar problems apart

Watch for a hard-bodied beetle actually chewing the plant. A beetle resting on a flower may be visiting for pollen.

Watch the beetle near a fresh hole before attributing the loss to it. Some beetles hunt other insects on foliage. Photograph the head, back and the shape of fresh bites. If feeding is confirmed on a small plant, collect visible feeders by hand where practical.

What to do now

Look at fresh feeding edges and the undersides of adjacent leaves. Note whether damage is confined to petals or also affects new leaves.

Check the leaf margin again after a beetle leaves. Photographing the newly missing piece can establish feeding even when the insect moves too quickly for identification. Save a close-up of any occupant alongside a picture showing the injury on the plant.

What to check next

Compare the next flush of leaves with a photo. If buds repeatedly fail to open, collect close-ups of both damage and beetle.
