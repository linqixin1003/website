White wax and mealybugs at stem joints

The photograph is still that wax-covered insect, not a stem. Along a stem, check where the leaf stalk joins it and under sheaths that are already dry and loose.

Inspect the specimen

Light the shaded side of the stem and move joint by joint. Lift a sheath that is already dry and loose, and do not tear one that is still green.

Check similar symptoms

A white film on the stem with no insect underneath is not this page. A scale cover is fixed and has none of the loose rear filaments.

Choose a response

Steady the soft tip with one hand and lift visible insects with a damp swab. Replace the swab when it is loaded with wax.

Track the next change

Choose one joint you have cleared and look weekly. Live insects at several joints mean a crevice is still occupied.

Host-specific check

Use this page for the stem joint and the dry sheath. An insect in a leaf axil belongs with the leaf guide page.

Examine the protected side of the node

Inspect the sheltered side of a leaf stalk and the folds around a nearby bud. Look for a distinct oval insect beneath the wax. After cleaning an accessible node, revisit that spot and also check a bud that was previously clean. New insects on the clean bud are better evidence of continuing activity than wax left behind on the old stem.
