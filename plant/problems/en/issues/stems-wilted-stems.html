Drooping flower stalks or wilting stems?

The hanging bloom is the obvious subject. The stalk is a green arc, not brown, and it is not broken at the soil.

Inspect the specimen

Feel from the neck to the base. Softness only at the neck belongs with the flower. Softness at the base while the upper stalk is still firm belongs with the stem.

Check similar symptoms

A dry spent bloom can hang from a stalk that is still straight. In this photograph the stalk is curved and still green.

Choose a response

Cut a stalk that is wet and soft at the base back to firm tissue and bag it. If only the bloom hangs and the stalk is firm, remove the bloom alone.

Track the next change

The next day, see whether the arc has become limp along its length and whether the bend has moved down to the soil.

Host-specific check

Use this page for the stalk of a bulb flower. Do not match a dead woody branch to this flower photograph.

Compare the flower stalk with its own leaves

A flower may hang naturally when it opens. Compare its stalk with the leaf stalks and other blooms on the same plant. A smooth bend under a heavy flower with firm leaves deserves observation first. When both stems and leaves lose support, check moisture inside the root ball and look for standing water before deciding to irrigate.
