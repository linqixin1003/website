Bronze egg clusters and gray nymphs on wilting squash

Squash bugs feed with piercing mouthparts and spend much of their time concealed underneath leaves or around the lower stems. A wilted pumpkin leaf is a reason to turn the foliage over. The combination of egg clusters, mobile nymphs and damage gives a more useful picture than wilt alone.

Where to look

Turn over squash and pumpkin leaves near the crown. Search the angles of the veins for compact groups of bronze-colored eggs. Check plants close to the one bearing eggs even if they have not wilted. Young nymphs can occur together in a small patch, so sampling one randomly chosen leaf per plant may overlook them.

What confirms it

Watch for gray, dark-legged nymphs or elongated brown adults moving onto stems when disturbed. Look under fallen leaves beside the plant as well.

Similar damage

Wilt alone has several causes. A hole with coarse frass at the stem base points toward a vine borer; visible feeding bugs and egg clusters point toward squash bugs.

What to do

Remove accessible egg masses and handpick groups of young nymphs on a small planting. Work around leaf undersides as well as the crown.

Follow-up

Check adjacent vines for newly laid clusters. Track whether fresh leaves stay upright while continuing to look for nymphs hiding low in the canopy.
