Fungal growth or mushrooms on potting mix

The fungus or mushroom is on the potting-mix surface, and the roots have not been turned out. Roots that are soft and slipping their skin belong with the root-rot guide page.

What to look for

Check whether growth is confined to the potting mix or reaches the base of the stem. Look at the condition of the newest leaves.

Possible causes and next steps

Fungi that break down organic matter can fruit in pots. Clear dead leaves and check how long the mix stays wet. Examine roots if the stem base softens.

How to tell similar problems apart

A mushroom or a patch of fungal threads on potting mix often feeds on decaying organic matter. Check the plant itself for wilting and the roots for soft tissue before attributing damage to the surface growth.

Look at what the growth is attached to. Threads across a piece of bark in the mix or a mushroom emerging from a buried wood chip can occur while nearby stems remain firm. Check the point where the plant enters the soil. A soft, discolored stem base or collapsing leaves deserve attention even if very little fungus is visible.

What to do now

Clear dead leaves from the pot, empty standing water from the saucer, and keep unidentified mushrooms away from children and pets.

Remove loose dead leaves and any accessible mushroom caps and stalks if children or animals could reach them. Wash your hands afterward. Picking a mushroom leaves its underground threads behind, so another flush does not by itself mean the plant has worsened. Avoid digging through fine roots or drenching the mix with disinfectant.

What to check next

If foliage and roots remain healthy, monitor the moisture cycle. If the plant wilts while the mix stays wet, inspect the roots rather than judging by the number of mushrooms.

After watering, note whether the pot drains and how long the deeper mix stays wet for this species. If the plant looks healthy and moisture suits it, continue ordinary care. Persistent wetness together with wilting calls for a root check even after surface mushrooms disappear.

Trace the base of a mushroom visually before removing it. A stalk arising from loose bark chips on top of the mix has a different origin from a shelf-like growth attached to buried woody tissue. If a woody plant is declining, record that attachment clearly.
