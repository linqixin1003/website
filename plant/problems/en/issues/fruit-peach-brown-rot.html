Tan cushions spread over a browning peach

A ripening peach may soften around a small injury and then develop many tiny buff-coloured cushions. Check fruit in contact with it as well as the affected peach.

What to look for

Inspect ripening peaches where fruit touch or the skin is bruised. Brown rot can start as a soft brown patch and expand until much of the peach is affected.

Check the pattern

Look closely at the surface for many small buff or grey tufts. Also check the tree for dried fruit still attached and for dead blossoms on twigs. These clues together are more informative than a bruise alone.

What to do

Remove rotting and mummified fruit from the tree and ground. Handle sound peaches gently at harvest and keep damaged fruit separate. Check the fruit that was touching each affected peach.

When to check again

At the next bloom, look for clusters of browned flowers and flag affected branches. If losses are repeated, ask a local fruit specialist about protection during bloom and before harvest in your climate.

Trace contact through a cluster

Before picking the rotten peach, notice which side faced its neighbour. Inspect the point of contact first, then turn the neighbour to look for a second injury. Remove dried fruit during winter as well, when branches are bare and old mummies are easier to see.

When sorting a basket for eating, discard a visibly moldy peach and any peaches that touched it. Mold may extend into soft flesh beyond the visible patch. Check the remaining peaches for decay and wash sound fruit before eating.
