Are the small covers on the stem scale insects?

The cover in the photograph is on a leaf, not on bark. On a stem, use that same translucent tan shape and look along soft twigs and the base of the leaf stalk.

Inspect the specimen

Covers on a stem are often denser and sit against the bark. Count those you can lift in a patch. Flakes in a dry bark crack are not covers.

Check similar symptoms

A lenticel is the stem's own rough point, colored like the stem, and it will not lift. A cover is a different color from the stem.

Choose a response

Wipe a few covers from soft green stem with a damp cloth. Do not scrape hard at a stem that has already formed cork.

Track the next change

Check the current season's shoot for new covers. Empty covers on old stem can remain into the next season.

Host-specific check

Use this page for the stem and the base of the leaf stalk. A lone cover in the middle of a leaf belongs with the leaf guide page.

Check activity underneath the cover

Covers may stay attached after treatment. On an accessible section, inspect beneath a few with a hand lens and distinguish dry empty covers from intact bodies. Look along the adjoining young shoot for newly settled specks. Compare the same length of stem on later visits so that differences in branch size do not masquerade as changes in scale numbers.
