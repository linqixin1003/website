Pale, stretched shoots

The photograph is a group of seedlings just through the soil. The white stems are thin and long, the seed leaves are small and yellow, and the ground is soil and litter.

Inspect the specimen

See whether the stem is a stretched hypocotyl and whether the seed leaves have opened into ordinary green leaves. This is the look of too little light after emergence.

Check similar symptoms

Yellow leaves on a seedling that already has several true leaves and a stem that is not stretched do not match this photograph. That is a color question on an older seedling.

Choose a response

Move the seedlings into brighter indirect light, or lower the lamp. Do not try to thicken the stems by extra water.

Track the next change

Shorter new joints and greener new leaves mean the light is now enough. Stems that stay white and long still need more light.

Host-specific check

Use this page for seedlings just emerged. When an older indoor specimen stretches between leaves, compare its true leaves and joints, not these seed leaves.

Read the next pair of leaves

Choose one shoot and note the distance between successive leaf attachments. After increasing light gradually, compare the next two intervals on that same shoot. Old stretched sections will stay long. Shorter new intervals with well-expanded leaves suggest improvement. If tiny pale leaves or dry patches instead appear on the window side, reassess how abruptly exposure changed.

Should a stretched shoot be shortened immediately?

First establish whether the plant normally climbs, and look for sound buds below the stretched section. Support a weak stem while adjusting its light. Before shortening an unfamiliar plant, check where that species can resprout on older wood. Removing the entire leafy section at once can leave little foliage to support replacement shoots.
