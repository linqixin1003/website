Dead rhododendron buds grow black bristles

Check the buds that stayed closed after neighbouring rhododendrons flowered. A dead, grey-brown flower bud covered in tiny black pins is characteristic of bud blast.

Look along the bud scales

Use side lighting and a hand lens. Fine black projections stand proud of the dry scales and cast tiny shadows. Look closely at an older affected bud if a freshly browned one has no visible projections yet.

Compare a live bud

A healthy flower bud is plump and opens as its scales pull apart. Frost can brown a bud internally without producing these black pins. A pointed vegetative bud that simply unfolds into leaves was never expected to flower.

Remove individual dead buds

Where you can reach them easily, snap off affected buds and discard them. Retain healthy buds and supporting shoots. Photograph a bristly bud before removing it if the diagnosis is uncertain.

Revisit at flowering

Mark one twig now and count the buds that actually fail to open next season. Leafhoppers may also be seen on rhododendrons, but their presence alone cannot tell you why a particular flower bud died.
