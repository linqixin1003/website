Soft, rotting stems

The stem is soft and may be wet. Brown spots on a stem that is still firm belong with the stem-spot guide page.

What to look for

Check whether softening starts at the soil line, and look for discoloration, sunken tissue or injury.

Possible causes and next steps

A newly emerged seedling with a water-soaked, constricted stem at soil level may have damping-off. In an established plant, soft tissue at the base calls for a separate check for crown or root rot. A wet pot alone cannot identify the cause.

How to tell similar problems apart

Support the shoot and look for a pinched, water-soaked section at the soil line. Avoid squeezing it repeatedly: a weakened seedling stem can fold under very little pressure.

Start at the point where the stem meets the mix and look upward, moving loose fallen leaves aside. Note the lowest firm section and whether the soft area is on one side or wraps around the stem. A shoot can still have green leaves even when the base is too weak to support their weight.

What to do now

Seedlings that have collapsed from damping-off rarely recover. Remove the collapsed seedlings and keep their tray water separate from healthy starts. For the next sowing, use clean containers and fresh seed-starting mix.

If the pot is wet inside a decorative cover, take it out and let it drain freely. Check that water can leave through the holes. A dry surface over a wet root ball is common enough to warrant checking below the surface before watering. Keep the stem base visible instead of adding mix to prop up a collapsing shoot.

What to check next

If several seedlings collapse, check where each stem bent. Collapse at the same narrowed point near the compost surface is a different pattern from tall seedlings leaning together toward a window.

If a single stem was bruised during transport, compare the edge with its original photo after it dries. A firm, stable edge can be left alone. Rapid softening into neighboring stems or collapse at the crown calls for prompt diagnosis; keep that pot’s runoff away from other plants.

Does the soft patch explain the whole plant’s collapse?

Compare the affected stem with an adjacent firm stem. A single bend injury usually centers on the point of impact. Several stems collapsing at the same soil level warrant inspecting the crown, drainage holes and water in the outer pot. During follow-up, feel the previously firm margin and neighboring bases; already decomposed tissue will not become firm again.
