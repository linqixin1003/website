Insufficient light

Pale, stretched shoots

This is one possible explanation. Compare the signs below with those on your plant.

What to look for

Compare the distance between nodes on new and old shoots. Check for leaning toward light.

Possible causes and next steps

Low light can cause elongated growth. Improve light gradually within the species’ tolerance.

How to tell similar problems apart

Compare the spacing between successive leaves with older growth on the same shoot. Long gaps and a lean toward a window suggest light is limiting.

Select one growing tip and follow it back to three or four successive leaf attachments. Compare the newest gaps with those formed before the plant was moved indoors or the season changed. Compare shoots of the same kind: a climbing plant searching for support can naturally carry a different pattern from a compact side shoot.

What to do now

Move the plant closer to suitable light gradually. Support weak stems while new growth develops.

Look from leaf height toward the window. A shelf lip, curtain or larger plant can shade this tip while the room looks bright to you. Move it out of that shadow first. Ease a shade-grown plant toward brighter conditions over several moves, watching the window-facing leaves for bleached patches before making another change.

What to check next

Old internodes will not shorten. Compare the spacing and firmness of the next several leaves.

Leave enough healthy leaves to support recovery. Wait for a few new nodes before deciding whether to shorten the old shoot, and check that the species branches after pruning. A palm with a single growing point can be permanently damaged by cutting off the top.
