Small stalked cups rise from soil under a camellia

After camellia flowers drop, part the litter and look at the soil. The photograph shows small cups growing from the ground, not the flowers themselves.

Inspect the specimen

The cups have short stalks, a pale rim and a deeper-colored interior. They stand in mulch or leaf litter, not on petals still attached to the shrub.

Check similar symptoms

A gray fuzzy coating stays on the spent flower and does not form open cups in the soil. The black pins of rhododendron bud blast sit on the bud, not on the ground.

Choose a response

Lift fallen camellia petals instead of leaving them in a wet mat. Bag surface mulch that already carries cups, and do not turn it back into the camellia root zone.

Track the next change

Part the litter under the same shrub after the next flowering. Browning petals alone, without cups in the soil, are not this sign.

Host-specific check

Search for these cups only beneath camellias. Brown fallen flowers elsewhere in the garden are not explained by this soil photograph.

Part the litter directly below an affected camellia without disturbing its shallow roots. Note whether the cups appear among last season’s flower remains. Photograph a newly browned bloom as well; a cup found under a different shrub cannot explain the petal damage by itself.

I found cups under my camellia, but the new flowers look fine. Should I strip the bed?

A cup-shaped fungus on a loose wood chip is not enough to diagnose petal blight. Look beneath the canopy for fallen camellia flowers and check newly open blooms over the next few days for brown patches that enlarge. If flowers remain sound, leave the roots and surrounding bed undisturbed. If petals repeatedly brown and drop, collect affected blossoms promptly and photograph both the flowers and the cups for identification.
