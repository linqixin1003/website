Identifying mealybugs on garden plants

The photograph is a single insect, not a leaf axil and not a whole specimen. The body is oval and waxy, with white filaments at the rear.

Inspect the specimen

Search the foliage for this outline: oval, segmented, waxy, with rear filaments. Start on the leaf underside and at the base of the leaf stalk, then photograph where you found it.

Check similar symptoms

Powdery mildew is a film that wipes off and has no insect underneath. Fuzzy mold has neither rear filaments nor short legs.

Choose a response

When only a few are present, lift the insect and the filaments with a damp swab and separate the pot from neighboring shoots.

Track the next change

Look at the base of a new leaf a week later. A live insect matters more than wax left at the old spot.

Host-specific check

Use this page to recognize the insect. The leaf guide page is for the axil, and the stem guide page is for the joint and the dry sheath.

After clearing a visible cottony cluster, inspect the joint just above it and the opposite side of the same leaf base. A mobile wax-dusted insect emerging from either recess shows the colony survived. Loose white fibers without insects are less informative, particularly just after cleaning.

When a pot plant touches its neighbor, check the leaves at the contact point before returning the cleaned plant to its place. A wax-covered insect in a second plant’s leaf axil changes the scope of the cleanup even if the original pot now looks clear.
