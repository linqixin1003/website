Scale insects

Shell-like bumps on stems or leaves

This is one possible explanation. Compare the signs below with those on your plant.

What to look for

Inspect bumps along veins and stems with a lens and distinguish surface shells from the plant tissue.

Possible causes and next steps

Scale insects can settle on stems and leaves. Separate the affected pot and inspect neighboring plants.

How to tell similar problems apart

Inspect several bumps with a hand lens. Regularly spaced pores in bark are normal; a scale insect has a distinct outline on the surface.

Look at both old wood and younger shoots to learn the normal bark pattern before testing a bump. Natural pores often repeat along a stem. Scale insects may cluster irregularly along veins, behind leaf stalks or on twigs. Inspect with a magnifier rather than scraping bark with a blade. Removing live bark can leave a lasting injury that is harder to assess than the original bump.

What to do now

Inspect leaf veins, petioles and woody joints. Gently lift one suspect scale on a sturdy stem rather than scraping the entire plant.

Check for sticky honeydew beneath the affected shoot. Soft scales can produce it, whereas armoured scales usually do not, so a clean shelf cannot rule out scale. On a small potted plant, photograph a short length of affected stem and a clean shoot on the same plant. Recheck those exact sites for new bumps or tiny moving crawlers. A dry shell at the original spot alone is a poor reason to repeat treatment.

What to check next

Empty covers may remain attached. Check for new insects on untreated growth before repeating any treatment.
