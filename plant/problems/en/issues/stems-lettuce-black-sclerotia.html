Large black lumps sit in rotting lettuce tissue

Open the softened lettuce. The black lumps in the photograph are embedded in the rotten tissue, larger than a pinpoint, and visible without a lens.

Inspect the specimen

The lumps are irregular and stuck into the soft tissue. They do not crumble away like specks of soil. Check whether the base of the head has collapsed while part of an outer leaf is still green.

Check similar symptoms

Gray fuzzy mold stays on the surface and does not form these large black lumps. The white cotton and tiny black grains at the base of an onion or garlic bulb are on an allium, and the grains are much smaller.

Choose a response

Bag the whole plant and take it off the bed. Do not chop it back into the soil, and do not eat the softened portion. Note which row it came from.

Track the next change

If heads in that row collapse at the base again, open one and look for the same large black lumps before planting lettuce there again.

Host-specific check

Record these large black lumps only in the rotten base of lettuce or a similar leafy vegetable. Black crusts stuck to potato skin belong with the potato black-scurf guide page.

Map the first collapsed lettuce heads

Mark the collapsed heads on a sketch of the bed. Compare where outer leaves touch soil on neighboring plants. A cluster in a low, shaded corner is a reason to check how long that spot stays wet. Remove detached black bodies along with the rotten head. Leave the hearts of healthy neighboring plants intact during inspection.
