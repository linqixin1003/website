Identifying longhorn beetles near trees

The photograph is the beetle on stone. There is no bark and no exit hole. The antennae are longer than the body and banded pale and dark.

Inspect the specimen

Use the antenna length and the white marks on the wing covers to recognize it. Holes and sawdust have to be found on a branch or trunk. This photograph does not show a larva inside wood.

Check similar symptoms

A leaf beetle has short antennae and rests on a leaf. A round hole with none of these beetles stays off this page.

Choose a response

Record and move a single beetle. Before moving timber, look at a cut face for fresh sawdust.

Track the next change

If a new hole in a trunk is pushing out dust, ask someone local who knows trees to look at the host. Do not decide to remove a tree from this stone photograph alone.

Host-specific check

Use this page to recognize the beetle. Holes and dust on a stem belong with the stem guide page, and both the hole and the dust need to be in the photograph.

Photograph the beetle and its exact resting place separately. An adult sitting on a wall or stone does not identify an infested tree. If fresh wood debris repeatedly collects beneath one opening on a nearby stem, photograph that opening and host for a local identification before cutting wood.
