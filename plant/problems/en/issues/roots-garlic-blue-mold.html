Blue-green mold beneath garlic bulb wrappers

A garlic head can look intact while one clove beneath the dry wrapper is shriveled and moldy. Check stored bulbs before separating cloves for autumn planting, especially where harvest or handling left bruises.

Inspect the specimen

Open a suspect bulb over a separate tray. Look for sunken areas and blue-green powder on damaged cloves, and note whether adjacent cloves remain firm. Keep the sample away from sound seed garlic.

Check similar symptoms

White rot centers on the bulb base and may produce tiny black bodies. Damage beginning at a torn clove wrapper with blue-green growth suggests a different problem. More than one decay organism can occur.

Choose a response

Discard affected bulbs from planting stock and minimize injuries while separating healthy cloves. Cure newly harvested garlic with adequate ventilation before storage, and keep the storage area free of discarded diseased material.

Track the next change

At the next sorting, compare bulb lots by harvest date and handling history. A rise in mold concentrated in one bruised lot calls for inspecting the lifting, curing and packing steps for that lot.

Host-specific check

Mark the damaged clove's position relative to the basal plate and wrapper tear. This makes it possible to compare where decay began across several heads in the lot.

Inspect cloves at the centre as well as those next to the torn wrapper. If several intact bulbs in the same storage box develop soft spots, check for damp packing and poor airflow. Keep visibly decaying bulbs away from garlic reserved for planting.
