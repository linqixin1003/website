Pale swollen galls on corn ears

Individual kernels or part of an ear swell into pale, irregular sacs.

What to check first

Look at how the swollen tissue joins the ear. Common smut can enlarge individual kernels or groups of kernels far beyond their neighbours. Young galls can have a smooth silvery covering. Older ones darken and split, exposing a black powdery interior. An intact gall need not be opened to document it.

How to tell similar problems apart

Separate swelling from missing grain. Poor pollination leaves gaps or poorly filled rows without a balloon-like structure. Feeding near the tip can leave chewed kernels and droppings. If only moldy discoloured kernels are present, take a close-up and consider a different ear problem.

What to do now

Inspect the stalk, tassel and other ears on the same plant, then look at neighbouring plants. Note whether only one ear is affected or damage is widespread. If removing a gall in a home patch, handle it gently before it breaks and collect loose affected material.

What to check next

For a recurring problem record the variety, location and any recent hail or mechanical injury. Keep this record when choosing seed and avoid damaging plants during garden work. Photographs of the gall and its position are more useful for a diagnosis than a sample of loose black dust.

Inspect the husk before stripping a damaged ear. Note whether a swelling pushes the husk apart from inside, or whether an animal has torn the husk and eaten rows of otherwise normal kernels. These can look alike from the outside.
