Leaf rot

Soft, decaying leaves

This is one possible explanation. Compare the signs below with those on your plant.

What to look for

Look for contact with damp soil or neighboring wet leaves and check for water-soaked margins.

For a container plant, lift the growing pot out of its outer cover and check the base. A dry surface can hide a wet lower layer. Note how long the mix has stayed damp since the last thorough watering and whether water has been collecting beneath the pot.

Possible causes and next steps

Remove collapsed tissue, water the mix rather than the leaves and check for decay at the petiole base.

How to tell similar problems apart

Press lightly beside the damaged area. Soft collapse can follow a bruise, chilling or decay; spreading softness is more concerning than a stable dent.

Trace the soft area back to the leaf stalk. A single damaged blade resting on wet mix can decay while the stem stays sound. Lift neighboring leaves off the surface. If softness extends into the crown or several stalk bases, inspect the whole base before trimming individual leaves.

What to do now

Remove detached, decaying material and clean tools before touching healthy tissue. Keep healthy parts away from leaking damaged tissue.

Clear away any fallen mushy blade touching another plant. Check the contact point underneath for a second soft patch when separating the neighboring leaves. At the next inspection, compare the moisture at root depth with your first reading.

What to check next

Watch where firm tissue meets the soft patch. If softness reaches the stem base or develops on several plants, seek identification promptly.
