Seedlings fall overnight with stems clipped at soil level

A row of newly transplanted vegetables can lose individual plants overnight when cutworm larvae feed at ground level. Start with the freshest fallen plant and keep its stem base intact for inspection. A seedling that never emerged is much harder to attribute to a particular insect.

Where to look

Inspect a fallen seedling before disturbing the soil. Look for a chewed end at or just below ground level and check if the neighboring plants still stand. Inspect adjacent standing seedlings before replacing the one that fell. Checking a little distance on both sides of the gap can reveal partial feeding on a stem that is still upright and easier to protect.

What confirms it

Search shallow soil and debris close to a fresh cut during the day. A curled caterpillar near the damaged stem is a stronger clue than an empty gap.

Similar damage

Damping-off leaves a constricted or soft stem at the base. Cutworm feeding removes stem tissue and can leave the upper seedling lying beside its stump.

What to do

Handpick any larvae found near fresh damage. Fit a collar around an individual young transplant with the lower edge penetrating the soil.

Follow-up

Return early the next morning and mark newly cut plants. Follow the advancing edge of injury instead of repeatedly digging around old gaps.
