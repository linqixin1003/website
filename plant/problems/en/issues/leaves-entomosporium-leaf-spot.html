Clusters of red leaf spots with pale centers

A photinia hedge may look brilliantly red during a healthy flush. The sign to investigate is a crop of separate spots developing within that new growth, especially when accompanied by thinning lower branches.

Inspect the plant

Inspect tender growth on susceptible rose-family shrubs such as red-tip photinia and Indian hawthorn. Small red dots can enlarge into pale-centered spots with dark red borders. Compare an exposed outer leaf with a sheltered leaf inside the hedge. Note whether small separate spots and larger merged patches are present on the same shrub.

Distinguish similar signs

Uniform reddish color on new photinia foliage is normal. Spots have discrete edges, and several may merge as older affected leaves start dropping.

Choose a response

Remove fallen infected leaves and redirect sprinklers away from foliage. Repeated shearing produces tender new leaves where the disease can continue.

Follow up

Check the next flush after rainy periods. If a hedge repeatedly thins despite less leaf wetness, have fresh spots identified before planning further treatment.

A detail to record

On red-tip photinia, mark a spotted red leaf and revisit it as the surrounding shoot turns green. The persistence and expansion of individual lesions can then be seen independently of normal leaf-color change.

Read a red-tip hedge from bottom to top

Photograph a lower branch beside a fresh shoot higher on the same shrub. Record missing leaves as well as spotted ones. Dense spots on retained foliage can understate the damage if the oldest diseased leaves have already fallen, especially in a hedge trimmed just before inspection.
