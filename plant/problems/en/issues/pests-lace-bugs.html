Lace bugs

Lace-winged bugs and pale stippling

This is one possible explanation. Compare the signs below with those on your plant.

What to look for

Look underneath stippled leaves for tiny lace-patterned wings and dark specks.

Possible causes and next steps

Lace bugs feed beneath leaves. Record the host and leaf age, conserve predators and check the next flush.

How to tell similar problems apart

Look for lace-like wings and dark varnish-like specks under a speckled leaf. Mites can produce similar damage above.

Pick a leaf with fresh pale dots beside one that still looks green. Turn each over and line up the damaged patch with the veins underneath. A cluster of dark specks directly beneath the bleached area is a good place to use a magnifier. A pale leaf with a clean underside warrants checking other causes, particularly if the whole shoot has faded evenly.

What to do now

Inspect the undersides of several leaves, especially near veins. Support each leaf when turning it to avoid snapping the petiole.

Assess how much green leaf area remains on the shrub. A few stippled leaves on an established plant call for a different response from widespread bleaching and early leaf drop. Avoid stripping all the speckled leaves from a stressed plant. On small plants, direct a gentle rinse beneath affected leaves if the foliage tolerates it. Compare the next leaves on that branch with those on a less affected branch.

What to check next

Track new growth for fresh stippling and living nymphs. Previously bleached patches will not turn green again.
