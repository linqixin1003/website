Bean or potato leaf tips develop triangular hopperburn

Potato leafhopper feeding can show up as a yellow or brown wedge at the tip of a leaf. Damage sometimes becomes obvious only after insects have been present for some time. Inspect green tissue near the scorch while the insects are still active.

Where to look

Select several leaves on a stunted bean or potato plant. Look for pale veins, curling and the tip-first pattern of yellowing, then check nearby plants of the same age.

What confirms it

Turn leaves slowly to spot pale green wingless nymphs that move sideways. Adults are small, bright green and wedge-shaped, and can jump or fly away before a leaf is picked.

Similar damage

Dry weather can accentuate hopperburn and also cause scorch by itself. Combine the shape of injury with repeated observations of live leafhoppers; a single brown margin is insufficient.

What to do

Scout young bean plants promptly because severe feeding early in growth can have lasting effects. If numbers remain high, consult local crop-specific guidance using actual insect counts.

Follow-up

Examine healthy expanding leaves for new injury and keep separate notes on nymphs and adults. Counting insects on several plants is more informative than watching damage expand on one already scorched leaf.
