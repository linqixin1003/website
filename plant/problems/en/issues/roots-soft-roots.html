Soft, rotting roots

Judge by a root that slips its skin and has no spring, not by how dark it is. A mushroom on the potting mix belongs with the surface-fungus guide page.

What to look for

Check the cachepot for standing water. Compare firm roots with sections whose outer layer slips off at a touch.

How to check a potted plant without causing more damage

Start with the pot in its usual position. Slip a finger into the mix below the dry surface and lift the inner pot to see whether it stands in water. A plant that wilts while the root ball is already wet calls for a different response from one with a light, dry root ball.

If the root ball slides out easily, support it from below and inspect the exposed sides. Healthy root color varies by species and potting mix. Firmness and an intact outer layer are more informative than brown color alone. Leave firm roots attached.

Possible causes and next steps

Waterlogged soil limits oxygen around roots. Empty the saucer, check the drainage holes and base the next watering on the moisture needs of the species.

How to tell similar problems apart

Root color varies by species and potting mix. Gently test a small exposed root: a soft outer layer that slips away is more informative than brown color alone.

If a small potted plant is wilting in damp mix, support the root ball and slide it out only if it releases readily. Look along the outside and bottom first. Compare several roots of similar thickness; healthy older roots may be brown and firm. A few dead roots near the pot wall do not show how much of the root system is affected.

What to do now

Check that the inner pot drains freely and empty trapped water from its cover pot. If repotting is necessary, handle firm roots gently and use clean tools for clearly decayed tissue.

If most roots are firm, let the pot drain and adjust watering to the plant. If the crown is rotting and most roots have broken down, discard the severely affected plant and its mix. Use a clean pot and fresh potting mix for a replacement.

What to check next

Look for firm new root tips and stable foliage. If softening reaches the plant base or wilting continues despite better drainage, seek a diagnosis while some healthy tissue remains.

Check recovery above ground between waterings. Stable leaves and new growth are more informative than repeatedly lifting fragile roots. If the crown softens or several plants sharing a watering tray decline together, separate the pots and seek help identifying the cause before reusing the mix.

Check whether water reaches the whole root ball. A dry central plug surrounded by wet loose mix can coexist with soft roots near the pot bottom. Compare both locations before changing the watering interval for the entire pot.

My plant wilts again after watering. Should I repot it today?

A newly repotted plant may still have a dry central plug inside damp outer mix. When the plug is dry and roots are firm, moisten it evenly and let excess water drain. When the whole root ball is wet and the plant wilts, check root and crown firmness before adding water.
