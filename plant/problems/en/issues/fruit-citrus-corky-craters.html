Raised, corky craters on citrus peel

A raised brown blemish on an orange or lime deserves a closer look when younger spots have a wet-looking edge or a yellow halo. Together these features can suggest citrus canker.

View the edge in profile

Hold the fruit at eye level without picking it. A mature lesion can protrude from the peel and have a rough, crater-like surface. Older lesions may lose their yellow halos, so compare marks of different sizes.

Inspect attached foliage

Check fruit, nearby leaves and young stems on the same tree. Photograph both sides of a spotted leaf in place if possible. A single old scar from rubbing against a thorn has fewer diagnostic clues than similar lesions on several organs.

Keep suspect material on site

Leave branches and fruit where they are and consult the local plant-health authority, especially if citrus canker has not been reported locally. Send clear photographs first and follow their directions before moving a sample.

Record new symptoms

Note the tree location, citrus type and branches with fresh lesions. Photograph the same cluster again if asked. Reporting a suspicion does not require identifying the bacterium yourself; laboratory examination can resolve lookalikes.

Check the side of a leaf opposite a suspect mark without detaching it. Record whether a raised lesion is visible on both surfaces and whether similar marks occur on the peel. This gives an inspector more information than several nearly identical fruit close-ups.

One lime has a corky mark. Do I need to remove the tree?

Look first at the exact place where the fruit rests. A single scar against a thorn or rubbing twig, with unmarked nearby growth, is worth photographing and revisiting. Several raised lesions on different fruits together with spots on both sides of young leaves warrant contacting the local plant-health service. Leave the tree and its fruit in place while awaiting instructions; photographs that show the contact point and nearby leaves help determine the next step.
