Olive patches turn corky on apples

Scab can leave a permanently rough patch on an apple and cause the growing skin to split. Leaf symptoms help connect an old fruit scar to the disease.

What to look for

Look for olive-coloured, slightly velvety patches on immature fruit. Older patches become rough and dark, and expanding fruit may crack where scarred skin fails to stretch.

Check the pattern

Check leaves on the same tree for olive or brown spots and early leaf fall. A smooth bruise beneath intact skin or a single hole leading to the core suggests a different cause.

What to do

Remove fallen leaves at the end of the season or shred them so they break down faster. The fungus can survive in leaf litter and start a new cycle when wet spring weather arrives.

When to check again

Inspect young leaves after wet spells the following spring. Record which varieties are affected before choosing replacement trees, since susceptibility varies and existing corky scars will remain visible until harvest.

Check under a typical lesion

Cut through a representative corky patch. A superficial scar with firm flesh beneath fits old scab. If brown tissue extends deeply or the flesh is breaking down, document that separately because decay can enter through an existing cracked lesion.

Compare the two sides of a lopsided apple. A corky patch acquired while the fruit was small may restrict growth on one side, leaving the opposite cheek fuller. A large misshapen area at harvest need not represent a recent surge of infection.
