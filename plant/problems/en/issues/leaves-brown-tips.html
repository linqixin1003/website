Dry brown leaf tips

Browning starts at the tip and moves inward. A soft black rim on a leaf in wet soil belongs with the black-margin guide page.

What to look for

Check for brittle tips, widening margins and crusty mineral deposits around the pot rim.

Feel the mix near the root ball before responding to a brown tip. If it is still moist, note whether the tips browned after a fertilizer application or a change of water supply.

Possible causes and next steps

Uneven moisture and salt buildup can injure tips. Review watering and feeding records and judge recovery from new leaves.

How to tell similar problems apart

Check where the damage begins. A brittle point on several long leaves warrants a look at water and fertilizer history; a soft patch halfway down one blade warrants inspection of that patch instead.

Look where green gives way to brown. Similar dry tips on many leaves, together with a crust on the pot rim, warrant reviewing fertilizer and irrigation water. A wet edge advancing down just one blade needs a closer look for localized injury or disease.

What to do now

If a crust has formed on the mix or pot rim, review how often fertilizer is added and whether excess water can escape. A crust is a clue to salt accumulation, not proof that salt caused every damaged tip.

Take the comparison photo before trimming a dead point for appearance. A freshly cut tip creates a new edge and can obscure the original pattern. At the next inspection, compare the moisture at root depth with your first reading.

What to check next

A dead tip stays brown after care improves. Monitor the next leaf as it expands and compare the length of the damaged tip on an older leaf with an earlier photo.

Check what happens at the next watering

With a repeatedly tip-burned potted plant, watch whether water drains into a saucer and is then reabsorbed. Empty accumulated drainage and note fertilizer frequency. Judge progress from the tips of newly expanded leaves; trimming the brown ends of old ones removes the comparison you need.

Why did a brown edge return after I trimmed the tip?

A narrow dry line exactly along a straight cut can simply be the cut edge drying. Leave it alone if uncut leaves are opening with healthy tips. If untouched new leaves also begin browning at their tips, examine moisture swings, crusting around the pot rim and recent fertilizer use before trimming again.
