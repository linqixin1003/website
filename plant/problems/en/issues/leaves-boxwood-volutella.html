Tan boxwood leaves with salmon-colored growth underneath

When one section of a boxwood hedge turns straw-colored, the fate of the leaves provides a starting point. Check whether they stayed attached, when the change began and whether the wood beneath them died back.

Inspect the plant

Inspect a single browned shoot before looking at the whole hedge. Volutella can leave dry tan leaves attached, with pale salmon-colored fungal growth beneath them in moisture. Take separate pictures of the discolored shoot, the leaf underside and a still-green stem at the boundary. Keep neighboring plants identifiable if more than one shrub is affected.

Distinguish similar signs

Rapid leaf drop and long dark streaks on green stems warrant a separate check for boxwood blight. Color change alone is also common after winter injury.

Choose a response

When only isolated shoots die, remove dead material in dry weather. Avoid working through the hedge while wet and clean tools between plants.

Follow up

Record whether the next shoot to brown keeps its leaves. If several neighboring plants decline quickly, ask a clinic how to submit material before pruning further.

A detail to record

In a clipped boxwood hedge, part the outer foliage at the affected patch and identify the main stem of that individual shrub. Interwoven shoots can make a single affected plant look like several.

Open the hedge at a damaged shoot

A clipped surface hides the transition from healthy to dead wood. Follow a tan shoot inward and compare leaves attached inside the hedge with exposed outer leaves. Photograph the point where the change starts before clearing dead material; the pattern disappears as soon as the hedge is sheared.
