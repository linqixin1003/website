Caterpillars

Caterpillars chewing foliage

This is one possible explanation. Compare the signs below with those on your plant.

What to look for

Follow fresh bites to find the larva; inspect folded leaves and the backs of stems.

Inspect the plant before washing or moving it. Start where the damage looks freshest and turn the leaf over without shaking the shoot. Include the attachment to the stem in your inspection. A hand lens or a close phone photo can reveal detail that a picture of the whole plant misses.

Possible causes and next steps

Record the insect and extent of feeding. A vigorous plant can tolerate some loss; monitor new growth before intervening.

How to tell similar problems apart

Look inside folded leaves for a soft larva and loose droppings. Slugs can make similar holes but may leave a shiny trail.

Look under the damaged leaf for fresh pellets and follow them upward to possible feeding sites. Identify the larva before removal, especially on a plant grown for butterflies. A few old holes with no new feeding may need no intervention on a vigorous established plant.

What to do now

Inspect buds and both leaf surfaces at dusk. Move an identified nuisance caterpillar with a tool; avoid handling hairy larvae.

Check a folded leaf or the underside of a nearby stalk if fresh pellets keep appearing but the exposed blade looks empty during daylight. Save a close-up of any occupant alongside a picture showing the injury on the plant.

What to check next

New holes with fresh droppings mean feeding continues. Photograph the larva before deciding whether to intervene further.
