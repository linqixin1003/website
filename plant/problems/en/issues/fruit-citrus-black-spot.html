Dark, sharply edged spots on ripening citrus

Inspect citrus as the rind changes colour. Small dark speckles and larger hard-edged marks can occur together. Citrus black spot is one possible explanation, especially when several fruits show similar lesions.

Compare individual lesions

Look for spots with a dark border and a lighter, sometimes slightly sunken centre. Photograph small early marks as well as mature ones. Black spot can take several forms, so not every lesion on one fruit will look identical.

Check the profile

Compare the spot with the surrounding peel under side lighting. A conspicuously raised corky crater with a wet margin points towards a different diagnosis such as citrus canker. Weather injury and other fungi can also blemish the rind.

Keep the fruit attached

Take one close photograph and one showing the whole tree. If black spot is suspected, contact the responsible plant-health service before transporting fruit, leaves or cut branches. Local authorities can advise whether an inspection is needed.

Compare at the same maturity

Record the citrus variety and stage of ripening, then inspect several fruits of comparable colour. Changes in peel colour can make existing spots easier to see. Keep the dates with each photograph when documenting apparent spread.

Note whether the marked fruit was grown on this tree or arrived in a mixed box of purchased fruit. A photograph of an imported orange can support a fruit inspection, but it cannot establish that trees growing at the place of purchase are affected.
