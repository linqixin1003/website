Scattered yellow or white spots

Yellow or white spots are scattered, with green between them. Dense pinpoint flecks belong with the stippling guide page, and marks that no longer grow belong with the scar guide page.

What to look for

Compare the spots with sun exposure and splash patterns, then inspect the underside for insects.

Choose a leaf with a small, separate lesion while the edge is still easy to see. Find a second leaf of similar age on a different shoot. Include a vein junction in a close photo so you can locate exactly the same area again after the leaf dries.

Possible causes and next steps

Light injury, sprays and feeding can bleach tissue. Review recent moves or treatments and remove an obvious stressor.

How to tell similar problems apart

Compare exposed and sheltered leaves. Bleaching on the sunward face suggests exposure, while fine dots with insects underneath suggest feeding.

Tilt a leaf under side lighting. Dried droplets can leave pale rings on top, while bleached tissue remains light when viewed from different angles. Check whether spots follow splash patterns or appear independently on sheltered leaves. Use plain water on one small spot to test for residue.

What to do now

Examine the underside and photograph the distribution of marks. Avoid immediately moving a shade-grown plant into full sun.

If residue clears, inspect the tissue underneath after it has dried. A removable surface mark and a persistent pale patch beneath it can occur together. If you request identification, include the plant name and both views of an early lesion while there is still green tissue around it.

What to check next

Check whether new pale marks develop on sheltered foliage as well. If so, document any insects or residue you find.

Test whether the spot is on or in the leaf

On a smooth leaf, moisten one small spot with clean water and blot gently. A dried spray droplet may lift or leave a ring; bleached tissue stays pale when the surface dries again. Leave a neighboring spot untouched for comparison and avoid rubbing fuzzy or delicate foliage.
