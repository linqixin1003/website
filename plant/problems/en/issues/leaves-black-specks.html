Clusters of tiny black dots

The black marks are tiny and crowded. Large black patches belong with the black-patch guide page, and a film that wipes off belongs with the sooty guide page.

What to look for

Use a lens to distinguish deposits from dots embedded in the tissue. Inspect the underside for slender insects.

Examine the surface under light coming from the side. Raised material casts a small shadow, while a flat change in the leaf itself usually does not. Look at the reverse of the same patch and at the leaf directly above it before deciding where the material came from.

Possible causes and next steps

Dots embedded in dead tissue may accompany leaf spot diseases; removable dots may be insect deposits. Reduce splash and document both sides of a leaf along with the whole plant.

How to tell similar problems apart

Look at the specks with a lens. Surface particles may lift off; dead spots embedded in the blade remain when wiped gently.

Inspect a dot with magnification. Loose grains beside silvery scratches warrant a search for feeding insects. Dots embedded in the center of an enlarging dead patch should be photographed intact for diagnosis. Scraping hard enough to break the surface destroys that distinction.

What to do now

Test a small area with a damp white cloth and compare both sides. Examine nearby leaves before deciding how widespread the problem is.

Photograph the cluster before wiping it. A picture showing the arrangement of dots within the spot preserves more information than a picture of loose black material. Keep a photo of the original surface so you can distinguish a new deposit from one missed during cleaning.

What to check next

If dots merge into dead patches or spread to new leaves, keep an intact sample for closer examination.

Look above the lowest marked leaf

Several marked leaves stacked vertically can collect material falling from higher shoots. Inspect the underside directly overhead for feeding insects or cast skins. If every speck sits inside a tan lesion instead, save one intact leaf with both fresh and mature lesions for examination.
