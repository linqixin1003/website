Daylily buds become squat and swollen

A single daylily stem may carry both slender buds and stunted swollen ones. Opening one misshapen bud is a practical way to check for feeding larvae.

What to look for

Compare several unopened buds on one flowering stem. An affected bud may be shorter and wider than its neighbours and fail to open while normally shaped buds continue developing.

Check the pattern

Open one abnormal bud over a tray and look between the folded flower parts for small pale larvae. A dry empty bud alone does not establish that daylily gall midge is present.

What to do

Collect swollen buds before larvae drop to the soil. Contain removed buds for disposal. Inspect plants you intend to divide and avoid moving suspect buds or attached soil to a new bed.

When to check again

Start checking as the earliest cultivars form buds next year. Note the first affected variety and whether later flowers open normally. This helps distinguish a short seasonal attack from a season-long flowering problem.

Separate broken buds from unopened ones

Inspect buds that still have an intact outer surface first. A bud already chewed open can contain visiting insects unrelated to the initial swelling. If you find larvae, photograph them inside the folds before tipping the contents onto your tray.

Daylily flowers open one after another. Compare a swollen bud with an intact bud of similar length on the same cultivar, rather than with yesterday’s collapsed flower. Affected early buds can coexist with a normal display later in the season.
